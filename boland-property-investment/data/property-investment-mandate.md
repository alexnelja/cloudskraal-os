# Property Investment Mandate: BRRRR Strategy

**Entity:** New Property Investment (Pty) Ltd
**Date:** 25 March 2026
**Strategy:** Buy - Refurb - Rent - Revalue - Refinance (BRRRR)
**Target Areas:** Paarl and Malmesbury, Western Cape, South Africa

---

## 1. Investment Thesis

Acquire 1-2 bedroom apartments and duplexes in **quality suburbs** of Paarl and Malmesbury for R500,000-R1,500,000 per unit. Target areas with professional tenant pools (working couples, young families, retirees) — NOT townships or high-risk areas. Apply a small refurbishment (R50-150K) to increase rental income and property value. Tenant the property from day one to be cash positive immediately. Revalue at 130-150% of purchase price. Refinance at 70-80% LTV to extract equity and redeploy into the next acquisition. Repeat.

**Tenant quality is the priority, not yield.** A 9% yield with reliable payment and low vacancy beats a 15% yield with 3 months arrears and eviction costs. Target suburbs where tenants are employed professionals who can afford and will pay rent consistently.

The Paarl-Malmesbury corridor benefits from:
- Proximity to Stellenbosch and Cape Town (N1 corridor growth)
- Growing demand from young professionals and families priced out of Cape Town
- Active university and agricultural workforce creating consistent rental demand
- Infrastructure investment (road upgrades, retail development)

## 4. Deal Criteria Checklist

### Must-Haves

- [ ] Purchase price under R1,000,000 (ideally under R800,000)
- [ ] 1 or 2 bedrooms (no bachelor units, no 3+ bed)
- [ ] Sectional title or freehold (no share block)
- [ ] Body corporate / levy max R1,500/month
- [ ] Structurally sound (no major structural defects, damp, or subsidence)
- [ ] Estimated gross rental yield of 10%+ after refurb
- [ ] Cash positive from month one (rent > bond + levy + insurance + rates)
- [ ] Within 5km of town centre (access to shops, schools, transport)
- [ ] Municipal services connected (water, electricity, sewage)
- [ ] Clear title deed, no caveats or disputes

### Nice-to-Haves

- [ ] Below-market price (distressed seller, estate sale, bank repo)
- [ ] Existing tenant in place (verify lease terms and rental amount)
- [ ] Parking bay included
- [ ] North-facing (natural light, lower energy costs)
- [ ] Pet-friendly complex (widens tenant pool)
- [ ] Near public transport routes
- [ ] Secure complex with access control
- [ ] Pre-paid electricity meter installed

### Deal-Breakers

- Body corporate levy exceeding R1,500/month
- Special levy pending or recently imposed (>R10,000)
- Sectional title scheme with <60% levy collection rate
- Major structural issues (cracked foundations, rising damp, roof replacement needed)
- Flood zone or known water ingress history
- Zoning restrictions preventing rental
- Title deed restrictions on letting
- Litigation against body corporate
- Asbestos roof (removal cost kills the deal)

## 5. Refurbishment Scope

### High-Impact Refurb Items (prioritise these)

| Item | Budget | Value Add |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Kitchen refresh** (new doors, countertop, handles, sink) | R15,000 - R35,000 | High |
| **Bathroom refresh** (re-grout, new taps, vanity, toilet seat, mirror) | R10,000 - R25,000 | High |
| **Full interior paint** (neutral colours: white/light grey) | R8,000 - R15,000 | High |
| **Flooring** (vinyl plank throughout) | R15,000 - R30,000 | High |
| **Light fittings** (modern, LED) | R2,000 - R5,000 | Medium |

### Medium-Impact Items

| Item | Budget | Value Add |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Curtain rails and blinds** | R3,000 - R6,000 | Medium |
| **Built-in cupboards** (if missing) | R10,000 - R20,000 | Medium |
| **Security gate** (front door) | R3,000 - R5,000 | Medium |
| **Exterior paint** (if freehold) | R5,000 - R15,000 | Medium |
| **Pre-paid electricity meter** | R2,000 - R4,000 | Medium |

### Refurb Budget Guidelines

- **Light refurb** (paint + clean + minor fixes): R50,000 - R70,000
- **Standard refurb** (kitchen + bathroom + paint + flooring): R80,000 - R120,000
- **Full refurb** (all of the above + cupboards + electrical): R120,000 - R150,000

**Rule:** Never exceed R150,000 refurb on a sub-R1M unit. If the refurb estimate exceeds R150K, walk away.

## 7. Financing Structure

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Bank** | Target best rate: FNB, Standard Bank, or Nedbank |
| **LTV** | 80% (100% bond applications via mortgage originator as stretch target) |
| **Rate** | Prime (currently 11.75%, March 2026) |
| **Term** | 20 years |
| **Entity** | New Property Investment (Pty) Ltd |
| **Personal surety** | Required by banks for Pty Ltd purchases |
| **Deposit source** | Cash reserves or equity from previous BRRRR cycle |
| **Refurb funding** | Cash (not financed - keeps bond low) |

### Bond Application Approach

1. Use a mortgage originator (e.g., BetterBond, ooba) for multi-bank submission
2. Apply to 4+ banks simultaneously
3. Negotiate rate: target prime minus 0.25% to prime minus 0.50% at scale
4. Ensure pre-approval before making offers

## 8. Levy and Body Corporate Limits

| Criteria | Limit |
|----------|-------|
| Monthly levy cap | R1,500/mo |
| Special levy | No special levy pending; historical max R10,000 acceptable |
| Reserve fund | Body corporate must have >3 months reserve |
| Levy collection rate | Must exceed 80% (ideally 90%+) |
| Insurance | Building insurance must be included in levy |
| Management company | Professional managing agent preferred over self-managed |

**Before making an offer, always request:**
- Last 12 months body corporate financials
- Minutes of last 2 AGMs
- Maintenance plan / 10-year schedule
- Outstanding debts or litigation
- Rule book (sectional title management rules)

## 9. Acquisition Process

1. **Source** -- Property24, Private Property, Facebook Marketplace, estate agent relationships, bank repos (FNB repossessed properties, Standard Bank Properties in Possession)
2. **Screen** -- Apply deal criteria checklist (Section 4 above)
3. **Inspect** -- Physical inspection + body corporate document review
4. **Offer** -- OTP conditional on bond approval and satisfactory building inspection
5. **Bond** -- Submit via mortgage originator to 4+ banks
6. **Transfer** -- Appoint conveyancing attorney, allow 8-12 weeks
7. **Refurb** -- Start refurb immediately on transfer (pre-plan with contractor)
8. **Tenant** -- Market on Facebook groups, Property24 rentals, local agents 2 weeks before refurb completion
9. **Revalue** -- Request bank valuation 3-6 months post-refurb (once tenanted)
10. **Refinance** -- Apply for further advance or switch bond based on new valuation
11. **Extract** -- Use extracted equity as deposit for next unit
12. **Repeat**

## 10. Exit Strategy

| Scenario | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| **Primary (hold)** | Hold indefinitely, service bond from rental income, benefit from capital appreciation and equity extraction |
| **Sell individual units** | Sell at market value once revalued. Target 30-50% capital gain after refurb. Suitable for units that underperform on yield. |
| **Portfolio sale** | Bundle 5+ units and sell as a tenanted portfolio to another investor at a slight discount to individual sale value but at a premium to purchase price |
| **Refinance and hold** | Preferred exit from each cycle -- refinance, extract equity, hold the asset |

### Target Portfolio Scale

- **Year 1:** 2-3 units
- **Year 2:** 5-8 units (using extracted equity from Year 1)
- **Year 3:** 10-15 units
- **5-year target:** 20+ units generating R40,000+/month net passive income

## 11. Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Interest rate increase | Stress-test at prime + 2% before buying |
| Vacancy | Target areas with <5% vacancy; maintain 1-month rent reserve per unit |
| Non-paying tenant | Strict vetting (credit check, payslip, employer confirmation, references); use rental management agency |
| Refurb overrun | Fix scope before starting; get 3 quotes; hold 15% contingency |
| Property value decline | Buy below market; refurb adds forced appreciation buffer |
| Body corporate dysfunction | Review financials and AGM minutes before buying |
| Regulatory changes (Rental Housing Act) | Stay compliant; use standard SAPS-registered lease |
