### 2.2 Small Business Corporation (SBC) Relief — Section 12E

**Qualification requirements:**
- All holders must be natural persons (no companies/trusts as members)
- Gross income ≤ R20M per year of assessment
- Not a personal service provider
- Not > 20% of receipts from investment income (interest, dividends, rent, royalties)
- Shareholders may not hold shares in another CC/company (with exceptions)

**SBC Tax Rates (2025/2026):**

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---------------|------|
| R0 – R95,750 | **0%** |
| R95,751 – R365,000 | **7%** |
| R365,001 – R550,000 | **21%** |
| R550,001+ | **27%** |

**Problem for Boerdery:** The R25.5M in loans receivable earning R2.04M interest means investment income is ~30% of total revenue (R2.04M / R6.7M). This **disqualifies** Boerdery from SBC status because investment income exceeds 20% of gross income. The director loans must be restructured (repaid or converted) before SBC status is achievable.

**If the director loans were eliminated:** Revenue ~R6.7M with no significant investment income → SBC would apply. Tax saving on the first R550K of taxable income would be approximately R80,000-R100,000 per year.

**SBC bonus — Section 12E accelerated depreciation:**
- Plant/machinery used in manufacturing: **50/30/20** write-off (vs normal 20% per year)
- Plant/machinery used in farming: Already gets favourable treatment under First Schedule
- Any new/unused manufacturing asset ≤ R7,000: 100% write-off in year 1

### 2.3 Turnover Tax (6th Schedule)

**Qualification:** Qualifying turnover ≤ R1M. Boerdery at R6.7M+ is far too large. **Not applicable.**

Could potentially apply to the Tourism entity if kept small and standalone.

| Turnover | Rate |
|----------|------|
| R0 – R335,000 | 0% |
| R335,001 – R500,000 | 1% |
| R500,001 – R750,000 | 2% |
| R750,001 – R1,000,000 | 3% |

### 2.4 Should the BKs Be Converted to (Pty) Ltd?

**Background:** Close Corporations cannot be newly registered since 1 May 2011 (Companies Act 71 of 2008, Section 13(1)), but existing BKs continue indefinitely. Conversion is optional.

**Arguments FOR conversion:**
- Access to Section 42 asset-for-share transactions (only available for "companies" — a BK can elect to be treated as a company under para 64 of the 8th Schedule but it's cleaner with a Pty Ltd)
- Easier to bring in external investors or partners
- Better perceived credibility with international buyers/certifiers (FSSC 22000, Rainforest Alliance)
- Corporate governance framework (Companies Act protections)
- Group relief under Section 45 (intra-group transactions) applies to "companies" — BKs qualify under expanded definition but it's simpler with Pty Ltds

**Arguments AGAINST conversion:**
- Conversion costs: CIPC filing ~R475, legal/accounting fees R5,000-R15,000 per entity
- No tax benefit — same 27% rate
- BKs have simpler compliance (no annual returns were required historically, though CIPC now requires them for BKs too)
- Conversion triggers no tax event if done under Section 44 (conversion of CC to company) or Section 64(1) of the 8th Schedule

**Recommendation:** Convert Alexenya BK to (Pty) Ltd when it begins active processing/export operations. The export certifications (FSSC 22000, Rainforest Alliance) and international buyer relationships favour a (Pty) Ltd. Cost is minimal (~R10,000-R15,000). Boerdery can remain as a BK — there's no practical disadvantage for a farming operation selling domestically.

## 3. Farming-Specific Tax Concessions

### 3.1 First Schedule to the Income Tax Act — Overview

The First Schedule provides special rules for persons carrying on "pastoral, agricultural or other farming operations." This applies to Cloudskraal Boerdery as the active farming entity.

**Critical point:** The farming concessions apply to the **entity that carries on farming**. If you split farming from processing, only the farming entity gets First Schedule benefits.

### 3.2 Paragraph 12: Development Expenditure

**Full deduction in the year incurred** for capital expenditure on:
- Dams, irrigation schemes, boreholes, wells, pumping plants
- Fencing
- Eradication of noxious plants/invasive alien vegetation
- Prevention of soil erosion
- Dipping tanks
- Building of roads and bridges on the farm
- Carrying of electric power from the main line to the farm
- **Planting of trees, shrubs, or perennial plants** (including rooibos replanting and vineyard establishment)

**Application to Cloudskraal:**
| Item | Estimated Cost | Deduction |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| New rooibos field establishment (planting) | R150,000-R300,000/season | 100% in year 1 |
| Vineyard replanting/new blocks | R100,000-R200,000 | 100% in year 1 |
| Fencing (sheep camps) | R50,000-R150,000/year | 100% in year 1 |
| Borehole drilling/maintenance | Variable | 100% in year 1 |
| Solar pump installations for irrigation | Capital cost portion | 100% in year 1 |
| Alien vegetation clearing | Variable | 100% in year 1 |
| Internal farm roads | Variable | 100% in year 1 |

**Note:** The 100% write-off for planting rooibos and vines is extremely valuable. Every new rooibos planting reduces taxable income dollar-for-dollar in year 1, even though the plants produce for 5-8 years.

### 3.3 Paragraph 12(1) — Limitation

Development expenditure deductions are limited to the taxable income from farming **before** claiming the development expenditure deduction. Excess is carried forward. So you cannot create a farming loss from development expenditure alone.

However, the carry-forward is uncapped and perpetual.

### 3.4 Farming Equipment — Depreciable Assets

**Under the First Schedule (Paragraph 12(1)(e)):** Farming machinery and implements get favourable treatment:
- General farming machinery: **50/30/20** write-off (or elected wear-and-tear per SARS Interpretation Note 47)
- Some items qualify for 100% write-off in year 1 if they fall under development expenditure

**Section 12B (not First Schedule):** Manufacturing assets in farming context:
- Plant or machinery used in farming/manufacturing: **50/30/20** over 3 years
- This covers tractors, harvesters, processing equipment

**Practical application — Cloudskraal equipment:**
| Asset | Cost | Write-off Method | Year 1 Deduction |
|-------|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| New tractor | ~R800,000 | 50/30/20 (Section 12B) | R400,000 |
| Tea cutting machine | ~R250,000 | 50/30/20 | R125,000 |
| Sifting/grading equipment | ~R300,000 | 50/30/20 | R150,000 |
| Forklifts | ~R350,000 | 50/30/20 | R175,000 |
| Irrigation system (capital) | Variable | Paragraph 12 (100%) | 100% |
| Sheep handling equipment | ~R80,000 | 50/30/20 | R40,000 |

### 3.5 Section 12B — Renewable Energy (Solar)

**Section 12B(1)(h):** Machinery, plant, implements, utensils and articles used in the generation of electricity from:
- Wind
- Solar (PV or CSP)
- Hydropower (< 30MW)
- Biomass

**Write-off rate: 100% in year 1** (for assets brought into use on or after 1 January 2016).

**Section 12BA — 125% Renewable Energy Deduction:**
Section 12BA provided a **125% deduction** for renewable energy assets. This was introduced as part of the load-shedding response measures. The 125% uplift applied to assets brought into use between 1 March 2023 and 28 February 2025. **This has now expired** for assets brought into use after 28 February 2025. The standard **100% first-year write-off under Section 12B(1)(h) still applies.**

**Application to Cloudskraal:**
If Boerdery installs a solar system at Garsland or Meulsteenvlei (say R500,000-R1,500,000):
- 100% write-off in year 1 under Section 12B
- At 27% tax rate, a R1M solar installation saves R270,000 in tax in year 1
- This is arguably the single best capex investment from a tax perspective

**Important:** The entity that owns and uses the solar installation claims the deduction. If Alexenya (processing) installs solar at the processing facility, Alexenya claims it. If Boerdery installs solar on the farm, Boerdery claims it.

### 3.6 Livestock Valuation — Paragraph 2-8 of First Schedule

Two methods available:
1. **Standard Values** (Paragraph 5-6): SARS publishes standard values per livestock category. Simple to apply, but generally lower than actual cost — meaning higher taxable income.
2. **Actual Cost / Market Value** (Paragraph 7-8): Uses actual purchase price or fair market value at year-end. More complex but can reduce taxable income if herds are growing.

**Standard values for sheep (approximate, varies by breed):**

| Category | Standard Value |
|----------|---------------|
| Rams | R600-R900 |
| Ewes | R300-R500 |
| Lambs | R150-R250 |
| Kapaters (wethers) | R250-R400 |

**Recommendation:** With R1.58M in sheep/wool revenue (2025), the livestock valuation method matters. If building the flock (more births than sales), standard values keep stock values low and defer tax. If reducing the flock (more sales than births), actual cost may be better. **Consult with your accountant on the current election — once chosen, changing method requires SARS approval.**

### 3.7 Income Averaging / Staggering — Paragraph 15-19

**Paragraph 15:** Where a farmer's taxable income from farming in the current year exceeds the average of the previous 5 years by more than R50,000, the farmer can elect income staggering.

**How it works:**
1. Calculate average farming income for the preceding 5 years
2. If current year exceeds average by > R50,000, the excess can be "staggered"
3. The excess is taxed as if it were spread over the current and preceding years (effectively lower marginal rate for individuals)

**Relevance to Boerdery:** Because Boerdery is a BK taxed at a flat 27%, income averaging has **no benefit for a company/CC**. It only benefits individual farmers on progressive rates. This would only matter if farming income was received personally by JAL Nel or F Nel.

### 3.8 Drought Relief — Paragraph 13-13A

**Paragraph 13:** If livestock is sold due to drought, stock disease, or stock reduction scheme:
- Proceeds can be deferred (spread over the current year and the following 5 years)
- Allows a farmer to avoid a spike in taxable income due to forced sales

**Paragraph 13A:** Where a farmer replaces livestock within the following 3 years, the replacement cost reduces the deferred income.

**Application:** The Northern Cape / Cederberg area is drought-prone. If Cloudskraal Boerdery ever has a forced livestock sale due to drought, this provision can defer potentially hundreds of thousands of rands in taxable income. **Keep this in mind — it requires an election in the year of sale.**

### 3.9 Which Entity Should Claim Farming Deductions?

**Only the entity that "carries on farming" qualifies for First Schedule treatment.** This must be Cloudskraal Boerdery.

If Alexenya does processing/export, it does NOT qualify for farming concessions — it is a manufacturer/trader. Alexenya would claim manufacturing deductions (Section 12C for plant/machinery: 40/20/20/20 write-off).

**Key principle:** Keep all farming activities (growing, harvesting, primary drying of rooibos) in Boerdery. Only move secondary processing (cutting, sifting, grading, blending, packaging) and export to Alexenya.

## 4. Processing & Export Entity (Alexenya)

### 4.1 Manufacturing Incentives

**Section 12I — Manufacturing Investment Allowance:**
Section 12I was a generous incentive (additional 35-55% deduction on qualifying buildings and 55-100% on qualifying machinery) administered by the DTIC. **The application window for Section 12I has closed** — no new applications have been accepted since the DTIC exhausted the R20 billion allocation. This incentive is **not available** for Alexenya.

**Section 12C — Plant and Machinery:**
Standard manufacturing write-off: **40/20/20/20** over 4 years.
Applies to plant/machinery used in a process of manufacture.

Rooibos cutting, sifting, grading, blending and packaging qualify as manufacturing. Equipment deductions:

| Asset | Cost (est.) | Year 1 (40%) | Year 2 (20%) | Year 3 (20%) | Year 4 (20%) |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Cutting machine | R250,000 | R100,000 | R50,000 | R50,000 | R50,000 |
| Sifting/grading line | R400,000 | R160,000 | R80,000 | R80,000 | R80,000 |
| Packaging equipment | R200,000 | R80,000 | R40,000 | R40,000 | R40,000 |
| Storage/warehouse fit-out | R300,000 | R120,000 | R60,000 | R60,000 | R60,000 |

**If Alexenya qualifies as SBC (Section 12E):** Manufacturing plant and machinery get **100% write-off in year 1**. Alexenya would need to meet SBC requirements (members are natural persons, turnover ≤ R20M, investment income ≤ 20%, etc.).

### 4.2 Export Incentives

**Zero-rated VAT on exports (Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act):**
Rooibos tea exported from South Africa is zero-rated for VAT. Alexenya would charge 0% VAT on export sales but can claim input VAT on all local purchases (equipment, services, packaging, transport). This creates a **permanent VAT refund position** — SARS pays you money back. Ensure robust documentation: bill of lading, customs declarations, proof of export.

**DTIC Export Incentives:**
- **Export Marketing and Investment Assistance (EMIA):** Reimburses costs of attending international trade exhibitions, trade missions, and marketing activities. Administered by the DTIC. Alexenya should apply as an "Emerging Exporter." Funding: up to R200,000 per exhibition.
- **Sector Specific Assistance Scheme (SSAS):** Co-funds export promotion in priority sectors (agri-processing qualifies). Up to 80% of costs for emerging exporters.

### 4.3 Transfer Pricing Between Boerdery and Alexenya

**Section 31 of the Income Tax Act** requires transactions between connected persons (which Boerdery and Alexenya would be, having common members) to be at **arm's length prices**.

**Practical approach:**
- Boerdery sells unprocessed/semi-processed rooibos to Alexenya at a price comparable to what Rooibos Ltd or CNAS pay for raw/semi-processed tea
- Currently Boerdery sells to Rooibos Ltd at ~R50-R80/kg (varies by grade). This is the arm's length benchmark.
- Alexenya processes and exports at R120-R250/kg (depending on grade and market)
- The processing margin stays in Alexenya
- **Document the pricing methodology** — keep a transfer pricing policy on file

**Example (using 2025 production of ~245 tonnes):**

| Step | Entity | Price/kg | Total | Taxable Income |
|------|--------|----------|-------|---------------|
| Raw rooibos production | Boerdery | Cost of ~R20-25/kg | Farming costs ~R5M | Farming profit ~R1.7M |
| Sale to Alexenya | Boerdery → Alexenya | R55/kg (arm's length) | ~R13.5M | Revenue to Boerdery |
| Processing + export | Alexenya | R150/kg (export price) | ~R36.8M | Margin: ~R23.3M less processing costs |

Note: These numbers are illustrative for a future state where all production is processed and exported directly. Currently most sales are to Rooibos Ltd and CNAS at bulk rates.

**Key risk:** If SARS determines the inter-company price is not arm's length, they can adjust the price and add a secondary adjustment (deemed dividend/loan). Penalties up to 200% of underpaid tax under Section 31(5).

### 4.4 Certification Costs (FSSC 22000, Rainforest Alliance)

- **Fully deductible** as business expenses under the general deduction formula (Section 11(a))
- FSSC 22000 certification: ~R150,000-R300,000 initial, R50,000-R100,000 annual surveillance
- Rainforest Alliance certification: ~R50,000-R150,000 initial, R30,000-R80,000/year
- Annual audit fees: deductible
- Staff training for food safety: deductible (Section 11(a) or Section 12H for learnerships)

**Section 12H — Learnership Allowances:**
If Alexenya registers learnership agreements with workers for food safety/processing skills:
- Additional deduction of R30,000-R50,000 per learner (depending on NQF level) on completion
- R40,000-R60,000 for annual allowance per registered learnership for disabled persons

## 5. Property Company

### 5.1 Entity Choice for Property Holding

| Factor | Company (Pty) Ltd | Trust | Individual |
|--------|------------------|-------|-----------|
| Rental income tax rate | **27%** | **45%** (retained) or beneficiary rate (distributed) | Up to **45%** |
| CGT on disposal | **21.6%** (27% × 80%) | **36%** (45% × 80%) if retained | **18%** (45% × 40%) max |
| Transfer duty on acquisition | Standard rates | Standard rates | Standard rates |
| Estate duty on death | Shares valued, not property directly | Property vests in trust (no estate duty if structured correctly) | Property in estate (20-25%) |
| Dividends tax on extraction | **20%** | N/A | N/A |
| **Effective rate on rental (extract to individual)** | **27% + 20% × 73% = 41.6%** | Distribute: beneficiary's marginal rate | Marginal rate |
| Interest deductibility | Yes (Section 24J) | Yes | Yes |

**Recommendation:** Use a **(Pty) Ltd company** for the property investment entity. Reasons:
1. Flat 27% rate on rental income vs up to 45% personally
2. Retained profits can be reinvested without triggering dividends tax
3. **BRRRR strategy (buy, rehab, revalue, refinance) works best in a company** because you can refinance and extract cash tax-free (loan from bank, not a distribution)
4. CGT at 21.6% effective is better than trust's 36%
5. Properties in a company are not in the individual's estate (estate duty planning)

**Caveat:** If you need to extract ALL profits annually, the combined company tax + dividends tax (41.6%) exceeds the individual rate at marginal rates below ~R1.8M. But if profits are reinvested into more properties, the company wins decisively.

### 5.2 Transfer Duty on Moving Existing Properties In

**Transfer duty rates (Property Valued in ZAR):**

| Value of Property | Rate |
|-------------------|------|
| R0 – R1,100,000 | 0% |
| R1,100,001 – R1,512,500 | 3% of value above R1,100,000 |
| R1,512,501 – R2,117,500 | R12,375 + 6% of value above R1,512,500 |
| R2,117,501 – R2,722,500 | R48,675 + 8% of value above R2,117,500 |
| R2,722,501 – R12,100,000 | R97,075 + 11% of value above R2,722,500 |
| R12,100,001+ | R1,128,600 + 13% of value above R12,100,000 |

**Example:** Transferring a R2M property from an individual to the property company:
- Transfer duty: R12,375 + 6% × (R2,000,000 − R1,512,500) = R12,375 + R29,250 = **R41,625**
- Plus conveyancing fees: ~R20,000-R30,000
- Total once-off cost: ~R62,000-R72,000

### 5.3 Section 42 — Asset-for-Share Transactions

**Section 42 allows a person to transfer an asset to a company in exchange for shares at a tax-neutral (rollover) basis.** This means:
- No CGT triggered on the transfer
- No transfer duty payable (exempt under Section 9(15) of the Transfer Duty Act)
- The company takes over the transferor's base cost

**Requirements:**
- The transferor must receive shares in exchange (equity shares)
- The company must be a "company" (Pty Ltd — BKs can qualify under expanded definition)
- The asset must be transferred at tax book value (or market value, with the rollover applying)
- Both parties must be South African tax residents

**This is the recommended route for transferring existing properties into the new property company.** It avoids transfer duty AND CGT on the transfer. The trade-off: the company inherits the low base cost, so when it eventually sells, CGT will be higher. But if the strategy is buy-hold-refinance (not sell), this is ideal.

**Example:** Property with market value R3M and base cost R1.5M:
- Without Section 42: CGT on R1.5M gain (individual: ~R270,000) + transfer duty (~R97,000) = **~R367,000 cost**
- With Section 42: R0 CGT + R0 transfer duty = **R0 cost** (deferred)

### 5.4 Capital Gains Tax on Property

CGT is triggered on disposal. In a buy-rehab-revalue-refinance strategy, **revaluation alone does NOT trigger CGT**. CGT only arises on actual disposal (sale or deemed disposal).

**Annual exclusion (individuals):** R40,000 per year
**Primary residence exclusion (individuals):** R2M gain excluded
**Companies:** No annual exclusion; 80% inclusion rate at 27% = **21.6% effective CGT rate**

### 5.5 Interest Deductibility — Section 24J

Interest on debt used to finance income-producing property is fully deductible under Section 24J, provided:
- The property produces taxable income (rental)
- The loan is used for the property (not mixed purpose)
- Interest is at a reasonable rate

**Application:** If the property company borrows R2M at prime + 1% (currently ~12.25%) to acquire/rehab a property:
- Annual interest: ~R245,000
- Tax saving at 27%: ~R66,000/year
- This makes leverage highly tax-efficient in a company

### 5.6 REIT Structure

**Not applicable at this scale.** REITs (regulated by JSE Listings Requirements and Section 25BB of the Income Tax Act) require:
- JSE listing
- Minimum property portfolio typically R500M+
- Compliance with collective investment scheme regulations
- Distribute 75%+ of distributable income

The Cloudskraal property portfolio is orders of magnitude too small for REIT treatment.

## 6. Tourism Company

### 6.1 Entity Choice

**Options:**
1. **Separate (Pty) Ltd** — 27% flat rate, clean separation, limited liability
2. **Inside the Trust** — income distributed to beneficiaries at their marginal rates
3. **Inside Boerdery** — farming concessions don't apply to tourism; complicates accounts
4. **Turnover Tax (6th Schedule)** — if revenue < R1M, simplified regime (0-3% on turnover)

**Recommendation:** If tourism revenue is expected to remain under R1M in the near term, consider a **separate (Pty) Ltd** that can elect for turnover tax under the 6th Schedule. Turnover tax on R500,000 would be ~R1,650 vs ~R50,000+ under normal tax. However, turnover tax comes with limitations:
- No input VAT claims
- No loss carry-forwards
- No capital allowances

If tourism revenue will grow beyond R1M or significant capex is planned (accommodation upgrades), use a **standard (Pty) Ltd at 27%** and claim capital allowances on the accommodation facilities.

**Moving income from Frances Nel to the company:**
Currently tourism income is in Frances Nel's name. She would need to:
1. Establish (or be a shareholder in) the tourism (Pty) Ltd
2. Transfer the tourism operation (client relationships, booking platforms, any physical assets) to the company
3. Declare rental income/management fees from the company if she owns the accommodation assets personally, OR contribute those assets to the company via Section 42

**Tax consequence of the shift:** Frances currently pays tax at her marginal rate (likely 26-36% depending on her total income). Moving to a company at 27% is roughly neutral, but retained profits compound tax-free until extracted.

### 6.2 VAT on Accommodation

**Commercial accommodation is subject to VAT at 15%.** (Section 7(1)(a) of the VAT Act)

If the tourism company's turnover exceeds R1M in any consecutive 12-month period, it **must** register for VAT (compulsory registration — Section 23(1)). Voluntary registration is possible from R50,000.

**Accommodation supplied to foreign tourists (non-residents):** Still VAT at 15% (domestic supply). The zero-rating for exported services does NOT apply to accommodation — the supply is deemed to take place where the property is located.

### 6.3 Tourism Incentives

- **Tourism grading (TGCSA):** Not a tax incentive but enables access to SA Tourism marketing channels
- **DTIC Tourism Support Programme:** Various grants for tourism infrastructure in rural areas (up to R5M for qualifying projects). Worth investigating for accommodation upgrades.
- **Section 12O — Films and Shows:** Not applicable
- **No specific tax incentive for tourism accommodation** at entity level

### 6.4 Municipal Rates

Using a farm property for tourism/accommodation may trigger **rezoning requirements** (agricultural → mixed use/commercial). This could increase municipal rates significantly. Check with the Hantam/Witzenberg municipality before formalising.

## 7. Inter-Company Transactions & Pitfalls

### 7.1 Section 31 — Transfer Pricing

**Applies to:** Transactions between connected persons (Section 1 definition) where:
- One is a resident and the other a non-resident, OR
- Both are connected persons and the terms differ from arm's length

**Note:** Section 31 was historically focused on cross-border, but **Section 31(2)(a) extends to domestic connected-person transactions** where the transaction results in a tax benefit. SARS can adjust prices in domestic related-party transactions under the general anti-avoidance rules (Section 80A-80L) even if Section 31 is narrowly interpreted.

**Connected persons (Section 1):**
- A BK/company and its members/shareholders/directors
- Two companies with common shareholders holding ≥ 20% (Boerdery, Alexenya, Winery all qualify)
- The Trust and its beneficiaries/connected persons
- JAL Nel, F Nel, and all entities in which they hold interests

**All inter-company transactions must be at arm's length. Document everything.**

### 7.2 Section 7C — Deemed Donations on Trust Loans

**Section 7C(1):** Where a natural person (or company connected to that person) makes a loan to a trust (connected person) at interest below the official rate, the difference is deemed a donation subject to donations tax at 20%.

**Official rate:** The "official rate" for Section 7C purposes is the repo rate + 100 bps. As at March 2026, repo rate is 7.50%, so the official rate is **8.50%**.

**Application to Nel Familie Trust:**
The Trust LENDS R485,000 TO Boerdery (not from a person to the trust), so **Section 7C does not apply in this direction**. Section 7C only applies to loans **to** a trust from connected natural persons.

**However:** If the director loan structure changes and loans flow to/through the Trust, Section 7C must be considered. Currently the director loans (R25.5M) are FROM Boerdery TO directors (individuals) — this is the reverse direction and Section 7C does not apply. But if the Trust were to make a low-interest loan to Boerdery or any entity, and the Trust has received money from connected persons, the chain could trigger Section 7C indirectly.

**Current structure: Section 7C is NOT triggered** on existing arrangements.

### 7.3 The R25.5M Director Loans at 8%

**Is 8% arm's length?**

The appropriate arm's length rate depends on context:
- SARS official rate of interest (for fringe benefits, Section 1): **Repo + 100bps = 8.50%** (March 2026)
- Prime lending rate: **11.25%** (March 2026, after recent cuts)
- Typical unsecured personal loan rate: 12-24%
- Typical related-party loan: SARS generally accepts repo + 100-200bps for secured; prime for unsecured

**Assessment:** At **8%** on unsecured loans to directors, this is **below prime (11.25%) and slightly below the official rate (8.50%)**. SARS could argue the rate is not arm's length for an unsecured loan to a connected person. The risk is that SARS imputes interest at the official rate or prime, increasing Boerdery's taxable interest income and/or treating the undercharge as a fringe benefit to the directors.

**Fringe benefit risk — 7th Schedule:**
If the loans to directors are at below-official-rate interest, the difference could be treated as a fringe benefit taxable in the directors' hands. At R25.5M × (8.50% − 8.00%) = **R127,500/year** in potential deemed fringe benefit per director (if split 50/50). The tax at marginal rate could be R40,000-R57,000.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Increase the rate to at least the official rate (currently 8.50%)** or, better, to prime (11.25%)
2. Formalize the loan agreements with written terms, repayment schedules, and security where possible
3. Ideally, **develop a plan to reduce the R25.5M loan balance** — this amount is material and will attract SARS scrutiny. It also blocks SBC status.

**Tax impact of R25.5M at different rates:**

| Rate | Annual Interest to Boerdery | Tax at 27% | Net to Boerdery |
|------|----------------------------|-----------|----------------|
| 8.0% (current) | R2,040,000 | R550,800 | R1,489,200 |
| 8.5% (official rate) | R2,167,500 | R585,225 | R1,582,275 |
| 11.25% (prime) | R2,868,750 | R774,563 | R2,094,188 |

The directors receive the interest and pay tax at their marginal rates. If they're in the 36-45% bracket, the interest received is expensive. **The circular flow of interest (Boerdery pays, directors receive, directors pay back portions) should be reconsidered. It may be more efficient to capitalise or forgive portions of the loans.**

**Loan forgiveness:** If Boerdery forgives director loans, this is:
- A deemed dividend under Section 1 (distribution to a connected person) subject to **20% dividends tax** on the amount forgiven
- Or potentially a donation subject to 20% donations tax
- On R25.5M, dividends tax would be **R5.1M** — so outright forgiveness is extremely expensive

**Better approach:** Convert director loans to equity (additional member's contribution in the BK). This may be treated as a capitalisation event and could be tax-neutral if structured correctly. Seek specialist advice.

### 7.4 Dividends Tax — Section 64E

**Rate:** 20% of dividends declared by a company/BK to its members/shareholders.

**Exemptions:**
- Dividends to SA resident companies (inter-company dividends are exempt under Section 64F(1)(a))
- Dividends to certain retirement funds/PBOs

**Application:** If Boerdery declares a dividend to JAL Nel and F Nel:
- R1M dividend → R200,000 dividends tax → R800,000 received
- Combined with the 27% corporate tax already paid, the effective rate is: 27% + (73% × 20%) = **41.6%**
- Compare to top marginal rate of 45% — the company + dividend route is slightly cheaper for high earners, but NOT dramatically so

**Strategy:** Retain profits in the company where possible. Extract via salary (tax-deductible to the company) up to the point where the marginal rate equals the combined company + dividends tax rate (~41.6%). Beyond that, retain and reinvest.

### 7.5 Donations Tax

**Rate:** 20% on first R30M cumulative donations; 25% above R30M (Section 54-64).
**Annual exemption:** R100,000 per natural person per year (Section 56(2)(b)).

**Trust distributions are NOT donations.** The conduit principle (Section 25B) means income distributed by a trust retains its character and is taxed in the beneficiary's hands. Capital distributions may trigger CGT in the trust (Paragraph 80 of the 8th Schedule).

### 7.6 GAAR — Sections 80A-80L

The General Anti-Avoidance Rule applies if:
1. An arrangement is entered into
2. It results in a tax benefit
3. The sole or main purpose is obtaining a tax benefit
4. It is abnormal or lacks commercial substance or creates rights/obligations not at arm's length

**Risk areas for Cloudskraal:**
- Splitting farming from processing between related entities (commercially justifiable — SARS would struggle to challenge this if genuine processing occurs)
- Interest rates on inter-company loans (must be arm's length)
- Trust distributions timed to minimise tax (SARS has challenged this pattern)
- Property transfers using Section 42 (specifically designed to be tax-neutral — low GAAR risk)

**Mitigation:** Ensure every structure has **commercial substance beyond tax**. The processing/export split has genuine business rationale (food safety certification, export licensing, operational efficiency). Document this rationale.

### 8.2 Transfer Duty on R12M Purchase

Meulsteenvlei at R12M:
- Transfer duty: R97,075 + 11% × (R12,000,000 − R2,722,500) = R97,075 + R1,020,525 = **R1,117,600**

**However:** If Meulsteenvlei is purchased as a going concern from a VAT-registered vendor:
- The sale may be zero-rated for VAT under Section 11(1)(e) of the VAT Act (sale of going concern)
- Transfer duty is then payable (transfer duty and VAT are mutually exclusive on property — if VAT applies, no transfer duty; if exempt from VAT, transfer duty applies)
- If the sale is VAT-inclusive: R12M × 15% = R1.8M VAT — but Alexenya can claim this as input VAT if VAT-registered

**Best outcome:** Structure as a VAT going concern sale → zero-rated VAT → transfer duty of R1,117,600 applies. OR if it's a standard VAT sale, Alexenya claims the R1.8M input VAT back (but needs the cash flow initially).

### 8.3 Current Lease Payments — R1.1M/Year

**Deductibility:** Lease payments on property used for farming/processing are deductible under:
- **Section 11(a)** — general deduction (expenditure actually incurred in the production of income)
- **Paragraph 12(1) of the First Schedule** — if the lease is for farming land

Currently the lease is likely in Boerdery. Once Alexenya acquires the property, the lease falls away and is replaced by depreciation, interest on the acquisition debt, and other property costs.
