## 1. Executive Summary

### Current Structure Problems

- **R25.5M director loans at 8%** generate R2.04M/year interest income, pushing investment income to ~30% of gross income and **disqualifying Cloudskraal Boerdery from SBC status** (Section 12E requires investment income below 20%). Lost saving: ~R80-100K/year.
- **Director loan rate (8%) is below the SARS official rate (8.50%)**, creating fringe benefit tax exposure of ~R127,500/year under the 7th Schedule.
- **All operations concentrated in one entity** (Boerdery) — no transfer pricing optimisation, no separation of farming concessions from manufacturing deductions.
- **No formal entity for tourism** — accommodation income flows through Frances Nel personally at up to 36% marginal rate instead of 27% corporate rate.
- **Alexenya Produksie BK is dormant** and remains a CC — needs conversion to (Pty) Ltd and activation for processing/export.
- **No Special Trust Type A for Angelique** — long-term care provision is unfunded and unstructured.
- **Estate duty exposure of R4.3M+** on Japie's death (Scenario B), with no liquidity to pay it. The R23.95M director loan becomes a taxable estate asset that Boerdery cannot repay in cash.
- **No Section 42 asset-for-share transfers utilised** — property movements between entities trigger unnecessary CGT and transfer duty.

### Step B: Convert Alexenya BK to (Pty) Ltd

| Detail | |
|--------|--|
| **What** | File CoR 15.2 (Conversion of Close Corporation to Company) with CIPC. Alexenya Produksie en Verwerkings BK becomes Alexenya Produksie en Verwerkings (Pty) Ltd. |
| **Why** | (1) International buyer credibility for FSSC 22000 and Rainforest Alliance certification. (2) Cleaner access to Section 42 asset-for-share transactions. (3) Companies Act governance framework. (4) BK conversion is tax-neutral under Section 44/Section 64(1) of the 8th Schedule. |

| **Timeline** | **May-July 2026** (CIPC processing: 4-8 weeks) |
| **Who** | Attorney files at CIPC; accountant updates tax registration; Alex coordinates |
| **Reference** | Companies Act Section 13(1) (CC conversion); Section 44 ITA (amalgamation transactions) |

### Step C: Transfer Meulsteenvlei to Alexenya via Section 42

| Detail | |
|--------|--|
| **What** | Once Alexenya is converted to (Pty) Ltd, transfer Meulsteenvlei property into Alexenya using a Section 42 asset-for-share transaction. The current owner (Boerdery or directors, depending on purchase structure) transfers the property to Alexenya in exchange for shares. |
| **Why** | Section 42 provides: (1) No CGT on the transfer (base cost rolls over). (2) No transfer duty (exempt under Section 9(15) of the Transfer Duty Act). Without Section 42, CGT + transfer duty could exceed R1M. |
| **Cost** | Conveyancing fees: R15,000-R25,000. SARS Section 9(15) exemption application: included in conveyancing. Share certificates and company records: R2,000-R5,000. **Total: R17,000-R30,000.** |
| **Tax consequence** | **R0 CGT. R0 transfer duty.** Alexenya inherits the original base cost of the property. CGT is deferred until Alexenya eventually sells (if ever — BRRRR strategy is hold-and-refinance). Shares issued to the transferor are "clogged" under Section 42(6) — cannot be sold within 18 months without clawback. |
| **Important caveat** | This applies to the transfer of the property *into* Alexenya. The initial *purchase* from the third-party seller at R12M still attracts transfer duty of R1,117,600 (this is unavoidable). Section 42 only applies to related-party transfers, not arm's-length purchases. **Strategy: Purchase Meulsteenvlei directly in Alexenya's name** to avoid a double transfer (buy in Boerdery then Section 42 to Alexenya). |
| **Timeline** | **Q3-Q4 2028** (aligned with Meulsteenvlei purchase timeline) |
| **Who** | Conveyancer with Section 42 experience; attorney; SARS for exemption certificate |
| **Reference** | Section 42 ITA; Section 9(15) Transfer Duty Act |

### Step D: Establish Transfer Pricing Policy

| Detail | |
|--------|--|
| **What** | Document and implement an arm's-length transfer pricing policy for all inter-company transactions, primarily the sale of raw rooibos from Boerdery to Alexenya. |
| **Why** | Section 31 of the Income Tax Act requires connected-person transactions at arm's length. Without documentation, SARS can adjust prices and impose penalties up to 200% of underpaid tax under Section 31(5). The Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) method is the most appropriate — Rooibos Ltd and CNAS purchase bulk rooibos at R50-80/kg, establishing the market range. |
| **Cost** | Tax advisor to prepare benchmarking study and written policy: R15,000-R30,000. Annual review: R5,000-R10,000. |
| **Tax consequence** | At R55/kg on 245 tonnes: Boerdery receives R13.5M revenue; Alexenya processes and exports at R120-250/kg. The processing margin stays in Alexenya. Both entities pay 27%, so the tax rate is the same — but the separation preserves First Schedule farming concessions for Boerdery and unlocks manufacturing deductions for Alexenya. If SARS challenges the R55/kg price, the benchmarking study against Rooibos Ltd/CNAS prices is the defence. |
| **Timeline** | **Before Alexenya begins active trading — Q2 2026** |
| **Who** | Agricultural tax specialist (prepare benchmarking study and policy); Alex implements; accountant reviews annually |
| **Reference** | Section 31 ITA; SARS Practice Note 7; OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (SA follows these) |

### 4.5 SARS Risk on Transfer Pricing

**Probability of audit:** Medium-High (related-party transactions with material values attract SARS attention, particularly where entities have common directors/members).

**Penalty if non-compliant:** Up to 200% of underpaid tax under Section 31(5) if SARS adjusts the inter-company price. On R13.5M in inter-company sales, even a 10% price adjustment = R1.35M revenue reallocation = ~R365K tax adjustment = potential penalty of R730K.

**Mitigation:** The R55/kg price is conservatively within the R50-80/kg market range. With a properly documented benchmarking study and annual reviews, the risk of a successful SARS challenge is low.

## 7. Special Trust Type A for Angelique

### 7.1 Legal Requirements

A Special Trust Type A is defined in Section 1 of the Income Tax Act as a trust created:

1. **Solely** for the benefit of a person with a disability
2. The beneficiary must be a person who, as a result of a **physical or mental disability**, is **incapacitated** from earning sufficient income for maintenance or from managing their own financial affairs
3. The disability must be certified by a **registered medical practitioner**
4. The trust deed must restrict the class of beneficiaries to **only the disabled person**

The trust deed must specify:

- **Sole beneficiary:** Angelique Nel
