# Section 42 — Asset-for-Share Transaction Guide

> Reference: Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, Section 42
> Transfer Duty Act 40 of 1949, Section 9(15)
> Disclaimer: Review with tax advisor before implementation.

---

## What It Does

Allows transfer of assets (properties, equipment, etc.) into a company **without triggering:**
- Capital Gains Tax (deferred, not eliminated)
- Transfer Duty (exempt under Section 9(15))

You receive **shares** in the company instead of cash.

---

## Requirements

1. Transferor receives **equity shares** (not cash, not a loan, not preference shares)
2. Shares issued by the **same company** that acquires the asset
3. Transferor holds **at least 10%** of equity shares after the transaction
4. Both parties are **SA tax residents**
5. The company must be a **company or CC** (not a trust)
6. The asset must be a **capital asset** (not trading stock — unless both parties elect otherwise)

---

## How It Works

```
BEFORE:                          AFTER:
┌──────────┐                     ┌──────────┐
│ Alex Nel │ owns Property       │ Alex Nel │ owns 100% Shares
│ (Person) │ (base cost R500K)   │ (Person) │ in Property Co
└──────────┘                     └──────────┘
                                      │
                                      ▼
                                 ┌──────────────┐
                                 │ Property Co   │
                                 │ (Pty) Ltd     │
                                 │               │
                                 │ Owns Property │
                                 │ (base cost    │
                                 │  R500K rolls  │
                                 │  over)        │
                                 └──────────────┘
```

- Property transfers at **tax base cost** (not market value)
- No capital gain triggered at transfer
- Company inherits the original base cost
- CGT only triggers when the **company eventually sells** the property

## Tax Impact — Worked Example

**Property:** 2-bed apartment in Paarl
- Original purchase price (base cost): R500,000
- Current market value: R850,000
- Unrealised gain: R350,000

### Without Section 42

| Tax | Amount |
|-----|--------|
| Transfer duty (sliding scale on R850K) | ~R11,250 |
| CGT on R350K gain (individual, 40% inclusion x marginal rate ~35%) | ~R49,000 |
| **Total tax cost** | **~R60,250** |

### With Section 42

| Tax | Amount |
|-----|--------|
| Transfer duty | **R0** (Section 9(15) exemption) |
| CGT | **R0** (deferred — company takes R500K base cost) |
| Conveyancing fees | ~R15,000-R25,000 |
| **Total cost** | **~R15,000-R25,000** |

**Saving: ~R35,000-R45,000 per property**

## Practical Steps

1. **Register Property Co (Pty) Ltd** at CIPC — R475
2. **Instruct conveyancer** — must be experienced with Section 42 transfers
3. **Conveyancer applies to SARS** for transfer duty exemption (Section 9(15) certificate)
4. **Deed of transfer** registered at Deeds Office — property transfers to company
5. **Share certificate issued** — you receive shares equal to market value of property
6. **Tax return filing** — Section 42 election on both transferor's and company's IT12/ITR14

---

## Important Caveats

1. **CGT is deferred, not eliminated.** When the company sells the property, it uses YOUR original base cost. The gain that was "rolled over" will eventually be taxed — at the company's 21.6% effective CGT rate (vs your personal rate which could be lower).

2. **Clogged shares.** The shares you receive are "clogged" under Section 42(6) — if you sell them within **18 months**, the rollover relief is clawed back.

3. **Cannot extract value as a loan.** If the company immediately lends you money equal to the property value, SARS may treat this as a disposal (substance over form).

4. **Only capital assets.** If the property is trading stock (you're a property dealer), different rules apply. For investment properties held long-term, Section 42 is clean.

5. **Multiple properties.** You can transfer multiple properties in a single Section 42 transaction. Each property's base cost rolls over independently.

6. **Trust as transferor.** The Nel Familie Trust can also use Section 42 to transfer property into a company. The trust receives shares, and the company inherits the trust's base cost.

## When NOT to Use Section 42

- If the base cost is close to market value (minimal gain to defer — just pay the small transfer duty)
- If you plan to sell the shares within 18 months (claw-back)
- If the transferor is not a SA tax resident
- If you need cash from the transaction (you receive shares, not cash)
