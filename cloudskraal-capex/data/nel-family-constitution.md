## Preamble

The Nel family has farmed the Bokkeveld plateau for generations. What began as a commitment to the land — to its soil, its seasons, and its people — has grown into Cloudskraal Boerderye, a rooibos farming operation spanning approximately 30,000 hectares in the Northern Cape of South Africa.

Cloudskraal is more than a business. It is a homestead, a livelihood, a way of life, and a legacy. The name itself speaks to who we are: people of the land, shaped by the vast skies and red earth of the Bokkeveld.

Japie and Frances Nel built Cloudskraal from modest beginnings into a diversified agricultural enterprise — rooibos production, processing, tourism, and now property investment. They did so through decades of hard work, careful stewardship, and a deep respect for the land and the community that depends on it.

This Constitution is our commitment to carry that legacy forward. It is a living document that records our shared values, governs our relationships with one another and with the business, and provides a framework for decisions that will shape the next hundred years of the Nel family's life on this land.

We write it not because we anticipate conflict, but because we believe that clarity, fairness, and foresight are the foundations upon which lasting families are built.

## Part I: Who We Are

### Article 1 — Family Vision

We, the Nel family, are custodians of Cloudskraal — not owners in the permanent sense, but stewards for those who will come after us. Our vision is to build a farming legacy that endures for a hundred years and beyond, rooted in:

- **Regenerative agriculture** — leaving the land in better condition than we found it
- **Financial discipline** — growing carefully, borrowing wisely, investing for the long term
- **Family unity** — supporting one another through honest communication and shared purpose
- **Community development** — creating opportunity for our workers, our neighbours, and the Bokkeveld region
- **Innovation within tradition** — embracing new methods and markets while honouring the knowledge that built this farm

### Article 2 — Family Values

The following values guide all family decisions, whether personal or commercial:

1. **Stewardship** — We do not consume the land; we tend it. Every decision must consider its impact twenty years from now.
2. **Hard work** — There is no substitute for effort. Privilege is earned through contribution, not inherited through surname.
3. **Integrity** — We deal honestly with one another, with our workers, and with our business partners. Our word is our bond.
4. **Care** — We look after our own. Every member of this family, regardless of their role in the business, is valued and provided for.
5. **Innovation** — We respect what works, but we are never afraid to try something better.
6. **Humility** — The land was here before us and will be here after us. We are grateful for the opportunity to work it.

### Article 3 — Definition of Family

For the purposes of this Constitution:

**3.1** "The Nel Family" comprises the blood descendants of Jacobus Albertus Lourens Nel (Japie) and Frances Nel, including:

- Their children: Alex Nel, Enya Nel, and Angelique Nel
- Any children legally adopted by Japie and Frances, or by their descendants
- The biological and legally adopted children of Alex, Enya, and Angelique, and their descendants in perpetuity

**3.2** Spouses of family members are welcomed into family gatherings and are entitled to attend the Family Assembly as observers. Spouses do not hold voting rights on family governance matters, nor do they hold ownership interests in the family entities by virtue of marriage alone.

**3.3** In the event of divorce, the former spouse ceases to be recognised as family for the purposes of this Constitution. Any ownership interests or entitlements acquired during the marriage are governed by the applicable marital property regime and, where relevant, the pre-emptive rights set out in Article 10.

**3.4** Step-children of family members may be included as beneficiaries of specific trusts or entities at the discretion of the Family Council, but do not automatically qualify as "family" under this Constitution.

**3.5** Any dispute regarding the definition of family shall be resolved by the Family Council, with a right of appeal to the full Family Assembly.

## Part II: How We Govern Ourselves

### Article 4 — Governance Structure

The Nel family governs its affairs through three bodies, each with a distinct purpose:

#### 4.1 The Family Assembly

**Composition:** All adult members of the Nel family (age 18 and older).

**Meeting frequency:** Annually, held at Cloudskraal during the December/January holiday period, or at such other time and place as the Family Council determines.

**Purpose:**
- Receive an annual report on the state of the family's business interests
- Elect members of the Family Council
- Vote on amendments to this Constitution
- Discuss family matters of common interest
- Celebrate the family's heritage and strengthen bonds between branches

**Quorum:** A majority of adult family members, present in person or by video call.

**Voting:** Each adult family member holds one vote. Decisions are by simple majority unless otherwise specified in this Constitution.

#### 4.2 The Family Council

**Founding composition (2026):**

| Member | Role |
|--------|------|
| Japie Nel | Chairman (advisory capacity from 2030) |
| Frances Nel | Member (advisory capacity from 2030) |
| Alex Nel | Member, Secretary |
| Enya Nel | Member |

**Future composition:** The Family Council shall consist of no fewer than three and no more than seven members, elected by the Family Assembly. At least one member from each family branch (the descendants of each of Japie and Frances's children) should be represented once the family grows to include multiple branches.

**Meeting frequency:** Quarterly, with minutes recorded and circulated to all adult family members within fourteen days.

**Responsibilities:**
- Develop and update family policies
- Oversee the succession pipeline
- Serve as the first point of mediation in family disputes
- Review the family's business interests and advise the Board of Directors
- Plan family gatherings, education programmes, and mentorship initiatives
- Oversee the Special Trust for Angelique (Article 13)
- Review this Constitution annually and propose amendments to the Family Assembly

**Decision-making:** Consensus is preferred. Where consensus cannot be reached, decisions are by majority vote of Council members. In the event of a tie, the matter is deferred to the next meeting or referred to the Family Assembly.

#### 4.3 Board of Directors

Each operating entity within the Cloudskraal group shall have a Board of Directors appropriate to its scale and complexity. The following principles apply:

- At least one independent, non-family advisor or director shall be appointed to the board of the primary farming entity (Cloudskraal Boerdery) by 2028
- Independent directors bring objectivity, industry expertise, and accountability
- The Family Council nominates family representatives to serve on entity boards
- Board appointments are based on competence, not entitlement
- The Board reports to the shareholders/members of the relevant entity, and provides an annual briefing to the Family Assembly

## Part III: Working in the Family Business

### Article 5 — Family Employment Policy

**5.1 Guiding principle:** Employment in the family business is a privilege earned through competence and commitment, not a right conferred by birth.

**5.2 Entry requirements for family members (applicable from 1 January 2031):**

- A minimum of two years of full-time work experience outside the family business, with at least one external employer
- Must apply for a legitimate, budgeted position — no positions shall be created to accommodate a family member
- Must go through the same interview and assessment process as any external candidate
- Must hold a relevant qualification or demonstrate equivalent practical experience

**5.3 Transition period (2026-2030):** Current family members actively involved in the business — namely Japie, Frances, Alex, and Enya — are grandfathered into their current roles. This recognises that the business is in a critical transition phase and that these family members are essential to its continuity.

**5.4 Compensation:**

- All family members employed in the business shall be compensated at market rates for their role, benchmarked against comparable positions in the South African agricultural sector
- No family premium and no family discount — the rate must be fair to both the employee and the business
- Annual performance reviews shall be conducted on the same basis as for non-family employees
- Bonuses and incentives shall be linked to performance, not to family status

**5.5 Family members who do not work in the business:**

- Retain their rights as beneficiaries of the Nel Familie Trust and any other entities in which they hold an interest
- Are entitled to attend the Family Assembly and receive annual financial reports
- Have voting rights on governance matters as set out in this Constitution
- Shall not interfere in day-to-day operational decisions

## Part IV: Ownership, Value, and Distributions

### Article 6 — The Business Entities

As at the date of adoption of this Constitution, the Nel family's business interests are held through the following entities:

| Entity | Nature | Primary Activity |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Cloudskraal Boerdery (Edms) Bpk | Close Corporation | Farming operations, rooibos production |
| Alexenya Produksie en Verwerkings BK | Close Corporation | Processing and export |
| Cloudskraal Winery (Pty) Ltd | Company | Currently dormant; reserved for future use |
| Nel Familie Trust (IT195/2000) | Inter vivos trust | Holds farm land (Plaas Klippe Rivier 630, ~30,000 ha) |

**Planned restructuring (2026-2028):**

The family intends to restructure its business interests into four distinct pillars:

1. **Farming** — Rooibos cultivation, livestock, and land management
2. **Processing & Export** — Value-added processing, packaging, and international sales
3. **Tourism & Hospitality** — Accommodation, agritourism, and experiences at Cloudskraal
4. **Property Investment** — BRRRR strategy property acquisitions in the Boland region

Each pillar should ultimately sit in its own legal entity, with ownership flowing through to the Nel Familie Trust or its successor structures as appropriate. The restructuring plan shall be developed in consultation with a specialist agricultural tax advisor and attorney, and approved by the Family Council.

### Article 7 — Ownership Policy

**7.1** Membership interests, shares, or beneficial interests in any family entity may not be transferred, sold, pledged, or encumbered without the prior written consent of the Family Council.

**7.2 Pre-emptive rights:** Before any family member may dispose of an interest in a family entity to a third party, the interest must first be offered to the remaining family members at the formula price determined under Article 8. The offering period shall be ninety (90) days.

**7.3 Right of first refusal:** If no family member exercises their pre-emptive right within ninety days, the interest may be offered to a third party, but at a price no lower than the formula price, and subject to the approval of at least 75% of the Family Assembly.

**7.4** No external party may acquire an ownership interest in any family entity without the approval of at least 75% of adult family members, voting at a duly convened Family Assembly.

**7.5** Interests in family entities do not form part of a marital estate unless explicitly agreed by the Family Council. All family members are strongly encouraged to enter into antenuptial contracts that exclude family business interests from the joint estate.

### Article 8 — Valuation Methodology

**8.1** For the purposes of any transfer, buy-out, or pre-emptive right exercise, the value of an interest in a family entity shall be determined using the **income capitalisation approach**:

> **Entity Value = Normalised Net Income / Capitalisation Rate**

Where:
- Normalised Net Income = average net profit after tax over the preceding three financial years, adjusted for non-recurring items and above- or below-market family compensation
- Capitalisation Rate = the prevailing prime lending rate in South Africa at the date of valuation

**8.2** If the parties cannot agree on the valuation, each party shall appoint an independent valuator, and the two valuators shall appoint a third. The median of the three valuations shall be binding.

**8.3** The valuation shall be updated every three years, or upon any trigger event (death, disability, divorce, or proposed transfer).

### Article 9 — Dividend and Distribution Policy

**9.1 Growth phase (2026-2032):** During the current growth and acquisition phase — which includes the R12 million Meulsteenvlei acquisition, the R25.6 million capital expenditure pipeline, and the entity restructure — the family commits to a reinvestment-first policy. Distributions to family members during this period shall be limited to:

- Market-rate salaries for family members employed in the business
- Tax-efficient distributions as advised by the family's tax advisor
- Emergency distributions approved by the Family Council

**9.2 Steady-state distributions (from 2032 or upon completion of the Meulsteenvlei acquisition financing, whichever is later):**

- A minimum of **30% of net profit after tax** shall be distributed to members/beneficiaries annually
- The remaining 70% (or such greater portion as the Board determines) shall be retained for reinvestment, debt reduction, and reserves
- The minimum distribution percentage shall be reviewed every three years by the Family Council

**9.3 Equal treatment of branches:** Distributions shall be made equally to each branch of the family (i.e., equal shares to Alex's line, Enya's line, and the Angelique Special Trust), regardless of which family members are actively involved in operations. Active family members are compensated through their salaries, not through preferential distributions.

**9.4** The Angelique Special Trust's share of distributions shall be paid directly into that trust and administered by its trustees in accordance with Article 13.

## Part V: The Next Chapter

### Article 10 — Succession Planning

**10.1 The transition at hand (2026-2030):**

Japie and Frances Nel have led Cloudskraal for over thirty years. Their transition from operational leadership to advisory roles is the most important succession event in the family's history, and it must be managed with care, respect, and clear milestones.

**Phase 1 — Shared Leadership (2026-2028):**
- Japie continues to lead farming operations with increasing delegation to Alex and Enya
- Frances continues to manage tourism and accommodation while documenting systems and relationships
- Alex focuses on financial strategy, export development, property investment, and the Meulsteenvlei acquisition
- Enya's operational role is defined and formalised in consultation with the Family Council
- An independent advisor is appointed to the Board of Cloudskraal Boerdery

**Phase 2 — Transition (2028-2030):**
- Alex and Enya assume day-to-day leadership of the business
- Japie and Frances step back from operational roles
- Japie and Frances retain their seats on the Family Council in an advisory capacity
- Knowledge transfer is prioritised: Japie's farming expertise and Frances's operational knowledge are documented

**Phase 3 — Advisory (2030 onwards):**
- Japie and Frances serve as advisors to the Family Council
- They retain life rights and retirement provisions as set out in Article 12
- Operational authority rests with the next generation

**10.2 Co-successor model:**

Alex Nel and Enya Nel are designated as the co-successors for the Cloudskraal group:

- **Alex** — Strategy, finance, export, property investment, and external relationships
- **Enya** — Operations, production, and such other responsibilities as agreed by the Family Council

The division of responsibilities between Alex and Enya shall be documented in a written agreement, reviewed annually, and approved by the Family Council. Neither co-successor has unilateral authority over the other's domain. Major decisions (defined as any commitment exceeding R500,000 or any matter affecting the family's long-term strategy) require joint approval or, failing agreement, referral to the Family Council.

**10.3 Future generations:**

For the third generation and beyond, the following pipeline applies:

| Stage | Timeframe | Activity |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Exposure | Age 10-18 | Holidays on the farm, attendance at Family Assembly, farm chores |
| Education | Age 18-24 | Tertiary education, encouraged in agriculture, business, finance, or a related field |
| Outside experience | Age 24-28 | Minimum two years of employment outside the family business |
| Entry | Age 26+ | Application for a legitimate position, subject to Article 5 |
| Development | First 5 years | Rotation through key areas of the business, mentored by senior family or non-family leaders |
| Leadership candidacy | After 5 years | Eligible for nomination to senior management or Board positions |

**10.4 Leadership election (future generations):**

When the time comes to select a new CEO, Managing Director, or equivalent leader from the third generation onwards:

- The Family Council shall form a selection committee comprising at least one independent advisor
- Candidates must meet the employment policy requirements of Article 5
- The selection committee shall recommend a candidate to the Family Council
- The Family Council shall approve the appointment by majority vote
- If no suitable family candidate exists, the family shall appoint a professional, non-family manager — this is a sign of maturity, not failure

Japie and Frances Nel have given their working lives to Cloudskraal. The family's obligation to provide for their retirement is absolute and takes priority over all other distributions and commitments.

This right is personal, inalienable, and may not be revoked by any family vote or business decision. The cost of maintaining the homestead in good repair shall be borne by the farming entity.

**11.2 Monthly annuity:** The farming entity shall pay Japie and Frances a monthly annuity of not less than R\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (to be determined in consultation with a financial advisor, based on their living expenses and the farm's capacity), adjusted annually by the greater of CPI or 6%. This annuity is a first charge on the farming entity's income, ranking ahead of all discretionary distributions.

**11.4 Family Council seats:** Japie and Frances shall retain permanent seats on the Family Council in an advisory capacity. From 2030 onwards, their role on the Council is advisory — they participate in discussion but do not hold a veto over operational decisions.

**11.6** These provisions may not be amended or reduced without the written consent of both Japie and Frances, or — after their passing — without a 75% vote of the Family Assembly.

Angelique Nel is a beloved member of this family. She has special needs that require dedicated, lifelong care and financial provision. The family's commitment to Angelique is unconditional and permanent.

**12.1 Special Trust Type A:** A Special Trust as defined in Section 1 of the Income Tax Act (a trust created solely for the benefit of a person with a mental or physical disability) shall be established for Angelique's benefit. - The trust shall receive an equal one-third share of all distributions from the family entities, as set out in Article 9.3

**12.3 Trustees:**

- At least one family member (initially Alex or Enya) shall serve as trustee at all times
- An independent professional trustee (a registered trust company, attorney, or accountant) shall be appointed as co-trustee
- The Family Council shall oversee the trust's administration and receive annual reports from the trustees
- No trustee may benefit personally from the trust's assets

**12.4 Care mandate:** The trustees shall ensure that Angelique receives:

- Safe, dignified, and comfortable accommodation
- Appropriate medical and therapeutic care
- Social engagement and meaningful daily activity
- Personal spending money and access to recreation
- Any other support necessary for her wellbeing and happiness

**12.5 Annual review:** The Family Council shall conduct an annual review of:

- Angelique's care needs and whether they are being adequately met
- The trust's financial position and whether funding is sufficient
- The performance of the trustees and care providers
- Any changes needed to the care plan

**12.6 Immutable provision:** No amendment to this Constitution may remove, reduce, or weaken the provisions for Angelique. This article may only be amended to improve or expand the care and financial provision available to her.

## Part VII: When We Disagree

### Article 13 — Conflict Resolution Protocol

Disagreement is natural. Unresolved disagreement is destructive. This article establishes a clear, escalating process for resolving disputes between family members on any matter relating to the family business, governance, or this Constitution.

**Step 1 — Direct Discussion (7 days)**

The parties to the dispute shall meet privately, in person or by video call, within seven days of the dispute arising. The goal is to reach agreement through honest, respectful conversation. No third parties are involved at this stage.

**Step 2 — Family Council Mediation (30 days)**

If direct discussion does not resolve the matter, either party may refer the dispute to the Family Council. A designated Council member (who is not a party to the dispute) shall facilitate a mediation session within thirty days. The mediator's role is to help the parties find common ground, not to impose a solution.

**Step 3 — External Professional Mediation (60 days)**

If Family Council mediation fails, the Family Council shall appoint an external professional mediator — preferably one experienced in family business disputes. The mediator shall conduct a structured mediation process. The cost shall be borne equally by the parties, or by the family entity if the dispute relates to business matters.

**Step 4 — Binding Arbitration (last resort)**

If mediation fails, either party may refer the dispute to binding arbitration under the Arbitration Act 42 of 1965. The arbitrator shall be a senior advocate or retired judge with experience in family and commercial law, appointed by agreement or, failing agreement, by the Arbitration Foundation of Southern Africa (AFSA). The arbitrator's decision shall be final and binding.

**Step 5 — Buy-Out Option**

At any stage during the dispute resolution process, any family member may elect to exit the family business by exercising a buy-out at the formula price determined under Article 8. The remaining family members shall have a period of twelve months to fund the buy-out, which may be paid in up to three annual instalments with interest at the prime lending rate.

**13.2** Throughout all stages, the parties commit to:

- Keeping the dispute confidential within the family
- Not involving employees, suppliers, or the public
- Continuing to fulfil their obligations to the business
- Acting in good faith and with respect for one another

## Part VIII: Growing the Next Generation

### Article 14 — Education and Development

**14.1 Financial literacy programme:** The Family Council shall ensure that all family members — including those not involved in operations — receive basic financial literacy education. This includes understanding financial statements, tax obligations, investment principles, and the economics of farming. The programme shall begin when family members reach the age of 16.

**14.2 Annual farm exposure:** Every member of the next generation shall spend a meaningful period on the farm each year — ideally during harvest season — from childhood through early adulthood. The purpose is not forced labour but connection: understanding where the family's livelihood comes from, respecting the land, and building relationships with the farming team.

**14.3 Mentorship programme:** Each family member entering the business shall be assigned a mentor — either a senior family member or a respected non-family professional — for their first three years. The mentor provides guidance, feedback, and a safe space to ask questions.

**14.4 Bursary fund:** The Nel Familie Trust shall establish a bursary fund for the education of family descendants. The fund may cover:

- Tertiary education (university or technical college)
- Postgraduate study
- Professional qualifications (CFA, CA, agricultural diplomas, etc.)
- Short courses, conferences, and study tours relevant to the family business

The Family Council shall determine the criteria, limits, and conditions for bursary awards. Academic performance, relevance to the family business, and financial need shall all be considered.

## Part IX: Our Responsibility to Others

### Article 15 — Community and Social Responsibility

Cloudskraal does not exist in isolation. Our workers, our neighbours, and the Bokkeveld community are part of our story, and we owe them more than a wage.

**15.1 Worker welfare:**

- Fair wages, benchmarked against agricultural sector norms and reviewed annually
- Safe and healthy working conditions, compliant with the Occupational Health and Safety Act
- Access to housing, water, and sanitation for permanent farm workers and their families
- Skills development and training opportunities
- Respectful treatment as a non-negotiable standard — any family member or manager who mistreats a worker will be held accountable by the Family Council

**15.2 BEE and enterprise development:**

- The family commits to meaningful compliance with the AgriBEE Sector Code
- Enterprise development: supporting emerging farmers in the Bokkeveld through mentorship, equipment sharing, or access to markets
- Supplier development: prioritising local and historically disadvantaged suppliers where commercially reasonable
- Skills development: investing in training for workers and community members

**15.3 Environmental stewardship:**

- Commitment to regenerative farming practices that improve soil health, conserve water, and protect biodiversity
- Responsible use of the Bokkeveld's unique fynbos ecosystem
- Progressive reduction of the farm's environmental footprint through solar energy, water harvesting, and waste reduction
- Participation in industry initiatives for sustainable rooibos production

## Part X: Living Document

### Article 16 — Amendment Procedures

**16.1 Scheduled review:** This Constitution shall be reviewed by the Family Council every three years. The review shall consider whether the document remains relevant, whether any provisions need updating, and whether new provisions are needed.

**16.2 Amendments:** Any amendment to this Constitution requires:

- A proposal submitted to the Family Council in writing, at least thirty days before the Family Assembly at which it will be voted on
- Discussion at a duly convened Family Assembly
- Approval by at least **75% of adult family members** entitled to vote

**16.3 Protected provisions:** The following provisions may not be amended except to improve or expand them:

- Article 12 (Provision for Angelique) — may never be removed or weakened
- Article 11 (Retirement Provisions for Japie and Frances) — may not be reduced without their written consent
- Article 13 (Conflict Resolution Protocol) — the requirement for a structured process may not be removed

**16.4 Emergency amendments:** In exceptional circumstances (such as a change in law that renders a provision unworkable), the Family Council may implement a temporary amendment by unanimous vote. This temporary amendment must be ratified by the Family Assembly within six months or it lapses.
