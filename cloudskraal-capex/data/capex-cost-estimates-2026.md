## 1. Tea Court / Drying Expansion

**Context:** Rooibos tea courts are large, flat, sealed surfaces (concrete or tar) where cut rooibos is spread thinly (5-10 cm deep) to sun-dry. Current capacity is a bottleneck at 245+ tonnes/season. The drying cycle takes 1-3 days depending on weather.

**Sizing:** Industry standard is approximately 100-150 m2 of court area per tonne of daily throughput. At peak harvest, Cloudskraal processes roughly 3-5 tonnes/day. A meaningful expansion would add 500-1,500 m2 of court area.

**Cost drivers:**
- Site preparation: leveling, compaction, drainage grading (1-2% fall)
- Base layer: G5 compacted gravel sub-base (150-200mm)
- Surface: 100-150mm reinforced concrete slab with steel mesh (most common) OR bitumen/tar surface
- Edge kerbing and drainage channels to prevent water pooling and contamination
- Access ramps for forklifts/wagons

**Cost breakdown per m2 (concrete court):**
| Component | Cost/m2 |
|-----------|---------|
| Earthworks, leveling, compaction | R80-R150 |
| G5 sub-base (150mm) | R60-R100 |
| Reinforced concrete slab (120-150mm, ref mesh) | R450-R700 |
| Surface finish (float/broom) | R30-R60 |
| Drainage channels and kerbing | R40-R80 |
| **Total per m2** | **R660-R1,090** |

Note: Tar/bitumen courts are cheaper (R350-R550/m2 total) but less durable and can taint tea in extreme heat. Concrete is industry standard for SAGAP/FSSC 22000 compliance.

| Scenario | Area | LOW | MID | HIGH |

**Recommendation for 245t/season:** A 1,000 m2 expansion at R875,000 (mid estimate) would meaningfully relieve the bottleneck. Combined with better scheduling, this should handle 300+ t/season capacity.

## 5. Wagon Turning Table

**Context:** A trailer steering/turning mechanism for agricultural transport wagons on narrow farm roads. Allows wagons to be rotated (typically 360 degrees) at loading/unloading points without requiring wide turning circles.

**Types available:**
1. **Manual turntable (ring bearing type):** Steel platform on ball-bearing ring, pushed by hand or pulled by tractor. Simplest option.
2. **Powered turntable:** Hydraulic or electric motor driven. More expensive but faster.
3. **Fifth-wheel steering conversion:** Converting a fixed-axle wagon to articulated steering. Different solution to the same problem.

**SA suppliers:** Henred Fruehauf (trailer components), Afrit, local agricultural fabrication shops. Many of these are custom-fabricated.

## 7. Soil Amendment Program

**Context:** Multi-year soil improvement for rooibos fields. Rooibos grows in acidic, sandy, nutrient-poor soils — but responds well to targeted amendments, particularly potassium, phosphorus, and organic matter. Active rooibos land is approximately 500 ha (across Cloudskraal 380 ha, Biekoes 283 ha, Glenridge 337 ha, Garsland 49 ha — roughly 530 ha active).

**Important note:** Rooibos does NOT tolerate lime/calcium (alkaline conditions). Amendments must maintain pH 4.0-5.5. This rules out standard agricultural lime application.

**Typical SA soil amendment costs for rooibos:**

| Amendment | Application Rate | Cost/ha | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|-------|
| Rock phosphate (Foskor) | 200-400 kg/ha | R800-R1,600 | Slow-release P source, rooibos-safe |
| Potassium sulphate (SOP) | 100-200 kg/ha | R600-R1,200 | Preferred K source — doesn't raise pH |
| Compost (organic matter) | 5-10 t/ha | R2,500-R7,500 | Delivered — expensive in Northern Cape due to sourcing/transport |
| Mulch/cover crop residue | N/A | R500-R1,500 | Growing cover crops and incorporating |
| Gypsum (calcium sulphate) | 500-1,000 kg/ha | R400-R800 | Improves soil structure without raising pH |
| Soil analysis (baseline) | Per sample | R400-R800 | Cedara/Bemlab — need 1 per 5-10 ha minimum |
| Trace elements (Zn, B, Mn) | Foliar or soil | R300-R600 | Based on soil analysis |

**Program cost per hectare per year:**

## 10. Electrical Distribution & Solar PV — Die Bult

**Context:** Solar installation for the farm processing facility. Load: 2x 24kW cutting machines + 15kW sifter = 63kW of installed motor capacity. Running approximately 4 hours/day, 5 days/week during season (Jan-Apr, ~16 weeks = 80 operating days). Plus off-season sifting.

**Load analysis:**
- Motors rarely run at nameplate simultaneously or at full load
- Diversity factor: ~0.7, so simultaneous demand ≈ 44kW
- Daily consumption: 44kW x 4 hrs = ~176 kWh/day
- Monthly (20 days): ~3,520 kWh
- Annual (seasonal, ~100 days): ~17,600 kWh

**Solar system sizing:**
- To offset 44kW peak demand: 50-60 kWp solar array
- Northern Cape irradiation is excellent: 5.5-6.5 kWh/m2/day (peak sun hours)
- A 55 kWp system produces ~275-357 kWh/day = more than sufficient
- However, motors have high inrush current — battery or grid support needed for starting

**Battery for load-shedding protection:**
- Need to run through 2-4 hour load-shedding windows
- 44kW x 4 hrs = 176 kWh usable storage (but impractical/expensive at this scale)
- Practical approach: 50-100 kWh battery buffer + solar, with generator backup for extended outages
- Alternative: 100 kWh battery (enough for ~2.5 hrs at 40kW)

**Solar PV pricing in SA (2025-2026):**
- Panels: R4-R6/Wp (Tier 1 panels have dropped significantly)
- Inverters: R2-R4/Wp (for 3-phase commercial — Fronius Symo, SMA Tripower, Deye, Sungrow)
- Mounting: R1.5-R3/Wp (roof-mount cheaper than ground-mount)
- Installation + commissioning: R2-R4/Wp
- Battery (LiFePO4): R4,000-R7,000 per kWh (Pylontech, BYD, Hubble, Dyness)
- **Total system cost (installed): R14,000-R25,000 per kWp (without battery), R20,000-R35,000/kWp (with battery)**

**System configurations:**

| Component | 50 kWp Solar Only | 50 kWp + 50 kWh Battery | 60 kWp + 100 kWh Battery |

| Solar panels (550W Tier 1) | R250,000-R350,000 | R250,000-R350,000 | R300,000-R420,000 |
