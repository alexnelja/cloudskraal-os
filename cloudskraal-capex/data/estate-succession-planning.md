## 2. South African Estate Duty Framework

### 2.1 Estate Duty Act 45 of 1955 — Key Provisions

**What is included in the estate?**

Every asset owned by the deceased at date of death (Section 3), including:
- Immovable property
- **Loan accounts owed to the deceased** (critical for the director loans)
- Deemed property: life insurance policies (Section 3(3)(a)), donations made in contemplation of death
- Accrued interest on loans
- Any property the deceased was competent to dispose of for their own benefit (Section 3(3)(d))

**What is NOT included (deductions)?**

| Exemption | Section | Description |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Abatement** | Section 4A | First **R3,500,000** of dutiable estate is exempt |
| **Spouse exemption** | Section 4(q) | Property bequeathed to or accruing to surviving spouse is fully exempt — no limit |
| **Trust bequests** | Section 4(h) | Bequests to certain public benefit trusts — limited applicability to family trusts |
| **Debts** | Section 4(a) | Debts owed by the deceased are deductible |
| **Funeral & deathbed expenses** | Section 4(b) | Deductible |
| **Administration costs** | Section 4(c) | Executor fees, Master's fees, etc. |
| **Rollover of unused abatement** | Section 4A(2) | If first-dying spouse does not use full R3.5M abatement, the balance rolls over to the surviving spouse (up to R7M total) |

### 2.2 Estate Duty Rates (Current)

| Dutiable Amount | Rate |
|-----------------|------|
| First R30,000,000 | **20%** |
| Above R30,000,000 | **25%** |

**Dutiable amount** = Gross estate - allowable deductions - R3.5M abatement

### 2.3 Capital Gains Tax on Death

Death triggers a **deemed disposal** of all assets at market value (paragraph 40 of the Eighth Schedule to the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962). This means:

- CGT is payable on the difference between base cost and market value at date of death
- **Individual inclusion rate:** 40%
- **Maximum effective CGT rate:** 18% (40% inclusion x 45% marginal rate)
- **Annual exclusion on death:** R300,000 (paragraph 5(1)(b))
- **Primary residence exclusion:** R2,000,000 (paragraph 45(1)(a))
- **Rollover to surviving spouse:** CGT can be deferred if assets pass to surviving spouse (paragraph 67A)

### 2.4 Executor Fees

Per the Administration of Estates Act 66 of 1965 and Guideline Tariff:

| Item | Fee |
|------|-----|
| Executor's remuneration | **3.5%** of gross estate value (excl. fixed property, reduced to 6% for fixed property in some tariffs — but 3.5% of gross is the default tariff) |
| Master's fees | Sliding scale, max R7,000 |
| Conveyancing (property transfer) | Variable, ~R30,000–R80,000 per property |
| Estate administration costs | Typically 5–8% of gross estate in total when all fees are included |

### 2.5 The Liquidity Problem for Farming Estates

This is the central threat to Cloudskraal:

- **Farm assets are illiquid:** Land, livestock, equipment, growing crops — none of these can be turned into cash quickly without severe value destruction.
- **Estate duty is payable in cash** within **12 months** of date of death (can apply for extension but SARS charges interest).
- **The executor must collect debts due to the estate** — including the R25.5M in director loans from Boerdery.
- **Boerdery does not have R25.5M in cash** to repay the loan. It would need to sell assets or borrow, potentially bankrupting the farm.

**This is the single biggest risk facing the Cloudskraal group.**

### 3.2 What Happens If Japie Dies?

**Step 2: Estate duty calculation (simplified)**

Assuming Japie's estate includes:

| Personal assets (vehicles, equipment, personal accounts) | R2,000,000 (est.) |

Deductions:
| Deduction | Amount |
|-----------|--------|

| Administration costs | ~R910,000 (3.5% of R26M) |
| Abatement | R3,500,000 |

**Scenario A: Everything left to Frances (Section 4(q) exemption)**

If Japie's will leaves everything to Frances:
- Section 4(q) exempts the entire bequest to the surviving spouse
- **Estate duty: R0**
- **But:** The loan problem merely shifts to Frances's eventual estate
- **Risk:** Frances now has a R23.95M asset (the loan) in her estate, plus her own assets
- **CGT:** Rolled over to Frances under paragraph 67A — no immediate CGT

This is the most common short-term strategy but it merely defers the problem.

**Scenario B: Everything left to children or trust**

If assets pass to children:
- No Section 4(q) exemption
- Dutiable estate after deductions and abatement: ~R21,500,000
- Estate duty at 20%: **R4,300,000**
- Plus executor fees: ~R910,000
- Plus CGT on deemed disposal of appreciated assets
- **Total cash required within 12 months: ~R5,200,000+**

**Where does R5.2M come from?** The executor must:
1. Demand repayment of the R23.95M loan from Boerdery
2. Boerdery has no cash to pay — it earns ~R6.7M revenue but has its own operating costs
3. Boerdery would need to borrow (if it can) or sell assets
4. A forced sale of farm equipment, livestock, or land destroys value
5. Land is in the Trust, not Boerdery — Boerdery's main assets are moveable farming assets and director loans

**This is the death spiral scenario.**

**Scenario C: Mixed — spouse inherits loan, children inherit other assets**

The will could specifically bequeath the loan account to Frances (exempt under 4(q)) while other assets pass to children or trusts. This requires careful will drafting but is the most practical short-term solution.

### 3.3 Mitigation Strategies for the Director Loan

#### Strategy 1: Life Insurance (Key Person / Estate Duty Cover)

**How it works:**
- Take out life insurance on Japie's life for at least the estimated estate duty + administration costs
- Policy owned by and payable to the estate (or a testamentary trust)
- Cash from policy pays estate duty, executor fees, and CGT
- The farm doesn't need to repay the loan immediately

**Considerations:**
- Policy must be sufficient: recommend minimum **R6M cover** (estate duty + admin + buffer)
- If policy is owned by deceased, its proceeds form part of the estate — but this is intentional (provides liquidity)
- If policy is owned by another entity (e.g., Boerdery), the proceeds go to that entity but Section 3(3)(a) may still deem it estate property
- **Key person insurance** on Japie payable to Boerdery would give Boerdery cash to repay part of the loan
- Cost estimate: R6M life cover for a 60-year-old male, ~R3,000–R8,000/month depending on health

**Recommended:** At minimum R6M life cover on Japie, R2M on Frances. Review annually.

#### Strategy 2: Systematic Loan Reduction

**How it works:**
- Japie systematically repays the loan over time (reduce the R23.95M balance)
- Each repayment reduces the estate exposure
- Can be done by offsetting against salary/drawings or actual cash payments

**Problem:** Japie likely doesn't have R23.95M in personal assets to repay. The loan may represent historical drawings that were never formally reconciled.

**Partial solution:** Convert a portion of the loan to salary/bonus each year, pay tax on it, and write off the loan. This has income tax implications (Japie pays income tax on the amount "repaid" this way).

**Recommended:** Begin reducing the loan by R2M per year through structured drawings. Target: reduce to R15M within 5 years.

#### Strategy 3: Loan Restructuring — Convert to Long-Term Debenture

**How it works:**
- Convert the demand loan into a long-term debenture (10–15 year repayment term)
- Argue a lower present value in the estate (discounted for time value of money)
- SARS may accept a discount if the terms are genuinely arm's length

**Risk:** SARS has become aggressive about discounting of connected-party loans. The Davis Tax Committee flagged this as an area for reform. Not reliable.

#### Strategy 4: Section 4(q) Spouse Bequest + Second-Death Planning

**How it works:**
- Will bequeaths the loan to Frances (tax-free under Section 4(q))
- Frances's will then plans for the second death with:
  - Life insurance to cover estate duty on her estate (which now includes the loan)
  - Gradual loan reduction during her lifetime
  - Bequest of the loan to a testamentary trust for children

**This is the most practical combined strategy.**

#### Strategy 5: Capitalisation of the Loan

**How it works:**
- If Boerdery is converted to a (Pty) Ltd, the director loan could be converted into share capital
- Japie would hold shares worth R23.95M instead of a loan
- Shares in a company are still an estate asset — but shares in an unlisted company can be valued at a discount (lack of marketability discount, minority discount)
- A professional valuation might support a 20–30% discount, reducing the estate value

**Tax implications of conversion:** Section 42 asset-for-share transaction could apply if structured correctly — potential for tax-neutral conversion.

**Recommended:** Investigate with tax advisor. Potential estate value reduction of R5–7M.

#### Strategy 6: Section 7C Implications (Anti-Avoidance)

**Important warning:** Section 7C of the Income Tax Act taxes interest-free or low-interest loans from trusts to connected persons as deemed donations. While the current structure has loans from Boerdery to directors (not from the Trust), any restructuring involving the Trust lending to individuals or companies at below-market rates will trigger Section 7C.

The current 8% interest rate on the director loans is above the official rate (currently 8.25% for 2025/2026 — this should be confirmed annually). If the rate drops below the official rate, Section 7C applies to loans from trusts.

## 4. Trust vs Company for Agricultural Land

### 4.1 Current Position

The Nel Familie Trust (IT195/2000) currently holds Plaas Klippe Rivier 630 (~30,000 ha in 3 portions). The question is whether this is the optimal structure.

### 4.2 Trust — Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Estate planning** | Assets in the trust are NOT part of any individual's estate for estate duty purposes (provided the trust is properly constituted and not an alter ego) |
| **Continuity** | Trust survives death of trustees — new trustees are appointed per the trust deed |
| **Creditor protection** | Trust assets are generally protected from personal creditors of beneficiaries (provided the trust is not the alter ego of the founder) |
| **Income splitting** | Trust can distribute income to beneficiaries in lower tax brackets (Section 25B conduit principle) |
| **No transfer duty on death** | Because the land stays in the trust — no transfer of ownership occurs when a trustee dies |
| **No deemed disposal on trustee death** | CGT is not triggered when a trustee dies — the trust is a separate taxpayer |
| **7th generation continuity** | The trust can hold land for generations without triggering transfer costs |

### 4.3 Trust — Disadvantages

| Disadvantage | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| **45% tax on retained income** | If the trust retains income (doesn't distribute), it pays 45% income tax — the highest marginal rate |
| **80% CGT inclusion** | Effective CGT rate of 36% (80% x 45%) — the worst of any entity type |
| **SARS scrutiny** | Trusts are under increasing scrutiny. The Davis Tax Committee (2015–2018) recommended taxing trust capital gains at the beneficiary level, limiting asset protection. While not yet enacted, reforms may come. |
| **Trust law reforms** | The Trust Property Control Act may be amended to require greater transparency, registration with Master, annual reporting |
| **Section 7C** | Loans from trust to connected persons at below-market rates trigger donations tax (anti-avoidance) |
| **Alter ego risk** | If the trust is run as Japie's "alter ego" (he makes all decisions, treats assets as his own), courts can "pierce the trust veil" — assets become part of his estate (see *Parker v Parker* and *Badenhorst v Badenhorst*) |
| **Limited beneficiary pool** | Current beneficiaries are Alex, Enya, Angelique — but what about their children, spouses? Trust deed may need amendment. |
| **Only 3 trustees** | With Japie and Frances as 2 of 3 trustees, the trust has a concentration risk. If both die, only GA van Rhyn remains — potentially below the minimum for decision-making depending on the trust deed. |

### 4.4 Company — Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **27% flat tax rate** | Significantly lower than trust retained income (45%) |
| **80% CGT inclusion but at 27%** | Effective CGT rate: 21.6% (vs 36% for trust) |
| **Separate legal personality** | The company exists independently of any natural person |
| **Perpetual succession** | Company continues regardless of death of shareholders or directors |
| **Easier to bring in investors** | Issue new shares, create different share classes (voting vs non-voting, preference shares) |
| **Governance framework** | Companies Act 71 of 2008 provides a clear governance framework (MOI, board, shareholders meetings) |
| **Flexible structuring** | Different share classes can address the "fair vs equal" problem between children |
| **SARS less suspicious** | Companies are the "normal" business structure — less scrutiny than trusts |

### 4.5 Company — Disadvantages

| Disadvantage | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| **Dividends tax** | 20% withholding tax when profits are distributed to shareholders (Section 64E) |
| **Double taxation** | 27% corporate tax + 20% dividends tax = effective rate of ~41.6% on distributed profits |
| **Shares are estate assets** | If Japie holds shares in the company, those shares are part of his estate |
| **Transfer duty to move land** | Moving land from the trust to a company triggers transfer duty (currently 8% on amounts above R2.3M for entities) — potentially R2M+ on R30M of land |
| **CGT on transfer** | Moving land out of trust triggers a deemed disposal — CGT on appreciation since acquisition |
| **No conduit principle** | Company cannot "flow through" income to shareholders the way a trust distributes to beneficiaries |
| **Loss of estate planning benefit** | Shares in the company are worth whatever the underlying assets are worth — back in the estate |

### 4.6 Comparative Tax Analysis

**Scenario: R1M of farming income, retained in entity**

| | Trust (retained) | Company | Trust (distributed to beneficiary at 31% effective rate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income | R1,000,000 | R1,000,000 | R1,000,000 |
| Entity tax | R450,000 (45%) | R270,000 (27%) | R0 |
| Beneficiary/shareholder tax | R0 | R0 (until dividend) | R310,000 (31%) |
| Dividends tax (if extracted) | N/A | R146,000 (20% of R730K) | N/A |
| **Total tax** | **R450,000** | **R416,000** | **R310,000** |
| **After-tax retained** | **R550,000** | **R584,000** (or R438K after dividends) | **R690,000** (in beneficiary's hands) |

**Key insight:** The trust is most tax-efficient when it distributes income to beneficiaries in low-to-moderate tax brackets. The company is more efficient if income is retained for reinvestment.

**Scenario: Sale of agricultural land (R30M market value, R5M base cost)**

| | Trust (capital gain) | Company (capital gain) |
|---|---|---|
| Gain | R25,000,000 | R25,000,000 |
| Inclusion rate | 80% | 80% |
| Taxable gain | R20,000,000 | R20,000,000 |
| Tax rate | 45% | 27% |
| **CGT payable** | **R9,000,000** | **R5,400,000** |
| **Saving in company** | | **R3,600,000** |

But if the company then distributes the after-tax proceeds:
- After-tax in company: R25M - R5.4M = R19.6M
- Dividends tax on distribution: R19.6M x 20% = R3.92M
- **Total tax (company + dividends):** R9.32M — slightly worse than trust

**Conclusion:** For land that will be held long-term and never sold, the trust is superior because no CGT is ever triggered and the land stays out of personal estates. For land that may be sold, the company is marginally better if proceeds are reinvested rather than distributed.

### 4.7 The "Alter Ego" Risk

South African courts have increasingly been willing to disregard the separate existence of a trust where the founder/trustee treats the trust as their personal asset. Key cases:

- **Parker v Parker [2005]** — trust assets included in estate for divorce purposes because husband treated trust as his own
- **Badenhorst v Badenhorst [2006]** — Supreme Court of Appeal confirmed that trust assets can be taken into account in divorce proceedings if the trust is the alter ego of a spouse
- **Jordaan v Jordaan [2001]** — trust veil pierced where trustee had unfettered control
- **WT v KT [2015]** — trust veil pierced in matrimonial case

**Risk for Nel Familie Trust:** If Japie makes all decisions regarding trust property unilaterally, treats the farm as "his farm," and the independent trustee (GA van Rhyn) merely rubber-stamps decisions, SARS or a court could argue the trust is Japie's alter ego. This would mean:
- Trust assets could be included in Japie's estate for estate duty
- Trust assets could be attacked by Japie's personal creditors
- The entire estate planning benefit is lost

**Mitigation:**
- Ensure proper trustee meetings are held and minuted
- Independent trustee (GA van Rhyn) must actively participate in decisions
- Appoint at least one additional independent trustee
- Never commingle trust and personal funds
- Trust must charge market-related rent to Boerdery

## 5. Succession Planning for Three Children

### 5.1 The Central Tension

Three children (Alex, Enya, Angelique) are beneficiaries of the Nel Familie Trust. The trust holds the land (~30,000 ha). The farm is a working agricultural operation generating income from rooibos, sheep, and grapes.

Potential future scenarios:

| Scenario | Likelihood | Complexity |
|----------|-----------|------------|
| One child wants to farm, two don't | High | Medium — but how to compensate non-farming children? |
| All three want to be involved | Low-Medium | High — governance needed |
| None want to farm | Medium | Extreme — what happens to 7 generations of heritage? |
| Two want to farm, one doesn't | Low | Medium-High |
| Children disagree fundamentally on direction | Medium-High | Crisis-level |

### 5.2 Principles of Fair Succession

**Fair does not mean equal.**

A farm cannot be divided into three equal pieces without destroying its viability. The child who farms contributes sweat equity, takes operational risk, and is tied to the land. The children who pursue other careers have liquidity, diversification, and freedom. An "equal" split (1/3 each of everything) creates:

- A deadlock situation where the farming child cannot make decisions without siblings' agreement
- Resentment if one child does the work and others benefit equally
- Pressure to sell if one child wants cash

**Recommended principle:** The farming child gets operational control and the bulk of agricultural assets. Non-farming children receive equivalent value through other assets, structured payouts, or preferential rights.

### 5.3 Mechanisms for Succession

#### A. Trust Deed Amendment

The trust deed of the Nel Familie Trust should be reviewed and potentially amended to include:

1. **Power to distinguish between beneficiaries** — allow trustees to distribute unequally based on involvement and need
2. **Sub-trusts or separate funds** — create separate "pools" within the trust for farming vs non-farming beneficiaries
3. **Right to occupy and farm** — grant the farming child a formal right to occupy and operate the farm
4. **Buy-out mechanism** — if a beneficiary wants to "cash out" their interest, define how this is valued and paid
5. **Dispute resolution** — mediation before arbitration before litigation
6. **Expansion of trustee pool** — add younger trustees, require at least 2 independent trustees

#### B. Shareholders Agreement (If Company Structure Adopted)

If agricultural properties move into a company, a shareholders agreement should include:

1. **Pre-emptive rights** — if a shareholder wants to sell, other shareholders have first right to buy at a fair value determined by independent valuation
2. **Tag-along rights** — if one shareholder sells, others can insist on selling on the same terms (protects minority)
3. **Drag-along rights** — if 75%+ shareholders agree to sell, they can compel the remainder to sell (prevents hostage situations)
4. **Deadlock provisions** — what happens if shareholders are 50/50 deadlocked? Mediation, then swing-vote director, then forced buyout
5. **Non-compete clauses** — the farming child cannot start a competing operation outside the family structure
6. **Valuation methodology** — agreed-upon formula for valuing shares (net asset value, earnings multiple, or independent valuation)
7. **Restraint on transfer** — shares cannot be transferred to non-family members without board approval

#### C. Buy-Sell Agreement

A buy-sell agreement funded by life insurance ensures that if one child dies or wants to exit:

- The remaining shareholders/beneficiaries have the right and the funds to buy out the exiting party
- Prevents shares/benefits falling into the hands of an ex-spouse, creditor, or unrelated heir
- Funded by cross-purchase life insurance policies between the children

#### D. Family Governance Charter

A non-binding but morally authoritative document signed by all family members that establishes:

1. **Family values and vision** — why the farm matters, the 7-generation legacy
2. **Decision-making process** — how family decisions are made (majority vote, consensus, or patriarch decides)
3. **Family council** — quarterly meetings of all adult family members to discuss farm strategy
4. **Entry requirements** — what qualifications/experience a family member needs before taking an operational role
5. **Compensation principles** — family members who work in the business are paid market-rate salaries; family members who don't work don't draw income
6. **Exit rights** — how a family member can exit gracefully without destroying the business
7. **Conflict resolution** — mandatory family mediation before any legal action
8. **In-law provisions** — spouses do not automatically gain rights in the business; separate property regimes required (ANC with or without accrual)

### 5.4 The "No Child Wants to Farm" Scenario

If none of the three children want to continue farming:

**Option 1: Professional Management**
- Hire a farm manager and retain family ownership
- Trust/company continues to hold the land
- Children receive income distributions
- Risk: professional manager may not have the same commitment

**Option 2: Long-Term Lease**
- Lease the farming operation to a third party (10–30 year lease)
- Family retains land ownership
- Steady rental income without operational risk
- Common in the Nieuwoudtville/Northern Cape farming community

**Option 3: Sale**
- Sell the farming operation and/or land
- CGT implications are significant (see Section 4.6 above)
- Proceeds distributed to beneficiaries
- Loss of 7-generation heritage — irreversible

**Option 4: Conservation / Carbon Credits**
- Convert portions to conservation land
- Access carbon credit markets and biodiversity stewardship agreements (CapeNature)
- Lower management intensity
- Potentially attractive tax benefits (Section 37D of Income Tax Act — donations of land to conservation)

**Recommended:** Discuss all four options openly as a family. Even if farming continues now, having a "Plan B" reduces anxiety and prevents crisis decision-making.

### 5.5 Marriage Regime Requirements

**Critical:** All three children should be married (or enter future marriages) under an **Antenuptial Contract (ANC) excluding community of property**, ideally **without accrual** for trust beneficiary interests.

Why:
- Marriage in community of property means the spouse automatically gets 50% of everything on divorce
- Marriage with accrual means the spouse shares in the growth of assets during the marriage
- An ex-spouse could potentially claim against trust distributions or company dividends
- The *Badenhorst* line of cases shows courts may include trust benefits in the accrual calculation

**Action:** Review marriage contracts of all three children immediately. If any child is married in community of property, this is an urgent problem.

### 6.1 Scenario 1: Japie Dies Suddenly

**Immediate effects:**
- Farm operations lose their primary decision-maker
- Boerdery loses a director — Frances is sole remaining director
- Trust loses a trustee — Frances and GA van Rhyn remain (2 of 3 — check trust deed for quorum)

**Operational mitigation:**
- [ ] Identify and document a "deputy farm manager" (whether family or employee) who can manage operations for 6–12 months
- [ ] Create an operations manual covering seasonal activities, key supplier contacts, employee management, compliance deadlines
- [ ] Ensure Frances or a designated family member has authority and knowledge to make time-sensitive farming decisions (e.g., harvesting, livestock health)

**Financial mitigation:**
- [ ] Life insurance on Japie: minimum R6M
- [ ] Will must be current and specifically bequest the loan account to Frances (Section 4(q) exemption)

### 6.2 Scenario 2: Frances Dies

**Immediate effects:**
- Admin, bookkeeping, tourism, and property management lose their primary person
- Boerdery loses a director
- Trust loses a trustee
- R1.55M loan enters Frances's estate (smaller problem)
- Tourism operations (currently in her personal name) may stall

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Document all administrative processes — accounting, tax submissions, compliance, insurance renewals
- [ ] Ensure at least one other person (family or employee) can access accounting systems, bank accounts, SARS eFiling
- [ ] Transfer tourism assets from Frances's personal name into a separate entity (Tourism Co) to avoid them being frozen in her estate
- [ ] Life insurance on Frances: minimum R2M (smaller estate exposure)
- [ ] Will to bequest loan account to Japie (Section 4(q))

### 6.3 Scenario 3: Both Die Simultaneously

**This is the catastrophic scenario.**

**Immediate effects:**
- Total operational vacuum — no directors, only 1 trustee (GA van Rhyn)
- Both estates must be administered simultaneously
- Combined R25.5M in loans enter the estates
- No one with authority to make farming decisions
- Risk of estate being administered by a bank executor who forces asset liquidation

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] **Joint/mutual will** or coordinated wills that nominate a trusted executor experienced in farming estates
- [ ] Trust deed must provide for emergency trustee appointment (e.g., "if fewer than 3 trustees remain, the remaining trustee(s) may appoint replacements without court application")
- [ ] GA van Rhyn must be briefed and prepared to step in as emergency decision-maker
- [ ] Appoint at least one additional trustee NOW — ideally one of the adult children (Alex) plus another independent person
- [ ] Key person insurance on both lives — joint life / second-death policy for estate duty on the combined estate
- [ ] **Second-death insurance policy** — pays out only when the surviving spouse dies — covers the deferred estate duty from the first death

### 6.4 Scenario 4: Children Disagree on Farm Direction

**Example:** Alex wants to invest in processing/export; Enya wants to sell portions of land for conservation; Angelique wants to continue traditional farming.

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Family governance charter (see Section 5.4)
- [ ] Shareholders agreement with deadlock provisions
- [ ] Neutral third-party mediator identified in advance
- [ ] Different business units in different entities (farming in Boerdery, processing in Alexenya, tourism in Tourism Co) so disagreements don't paralyze everything
- [ ] Board of directors/advisors including at least one independent non-family member

### 6.5 Scenario 5: One Child Wants to Cash Out

**Example:** Angelique lives in Johannesburg, has no interest in farming, and wants her "share" of the trust in cash.

**Problem:** The trust holds land worth ~R30M+ but has minimal cash. Selling land to pay one beneficiary would trigger CGT and reduce the farming operation.

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Trust deed should clarify that beneficiaries have no automatic right to demand capital distribution
- [ ] Create a buy-out mechanism: the farming entity (or other beneficiaries) can purchase the exiting beneficiary's interest over a 5–10 year period
- [ ] Fund the buy-out through farm profits, life insurance, or a sinking fund
- [ ] Alternatively: restructure to company with different share classes — non-farming children get preference shares (dividend income) while farming child gets ordinary shares (control + residual value)
- [ ] Pre-agree a valuation methodology that accounts for the illiquidity and agricultural use of the land (agricultural valuations are typically lower than market valuations)

### 6.6 Scenario 6: Divorce of a Beneficiary

**Risk:** An ex-spouse claims that the beneficiary's interest in the Nel Familie Trust constitutes an asset in the matrimonial estate.

**Legal position:**
- A beneficiary of a discretionary trust has a *spes* (hope/expectation) of benefit, not a vested right
- However, South African courts have increasingly been willing to consider trust benefits as part of the accrual estate (*Badenhorst v Badenhorst*; *RB v TL* [2021])
- If the trust regularly distributes to the beneficiary, the court may treat future distributions as an asset

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] All children should be married ANC, ideally without accrual for trust interests
- [ ] Trust deed should include a clause that benefits are discretionary and not guaranteed
- [ ] Trustees should vary distributions (not pay the same amount every year like a salary)
- [ ] Trust should keep impeccable records showing independent trustee decision-making
- [ ] Consider a "spendthrift" clause in the trust deed: beneficiary cannot cede, pledge, or encumber their interest

### 6.7 Scenario 7: Business Creditors Force Sale

**Current liabilities of Boerdery:** R6.3M in loans from group entities (Alexenya R4.2M, Winery R2.08M, Trust R485K) plus bank facilities.

**Risk:** If Boerdery defaults on external debt, creditors could attach Boerdery's assets. However:
- Trust property is NOT Boerdery property — creditors of Boerdery cannot attach the farm land (held in trust)
- This is a key advantage of keeping land in the trust
- Boerdery's main attachable assets are equipment, livestock, debtors, and the director loan accounts

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Keep the land in a separate entity (trust or property company) — NEVER in the operating entity
- [ ] Inter-company loans should be documented with proper loan agreements
- [ ] Avoid cross-suretyship where the trust guarantees Boerdery's debts (this would allow creditors to attach trust assets)
- [ ] Maintain adequate insurance on farm assets (equipment, livestock, crops, buildings)

### 6.8 Scenario 8: SARS Claims Against Estate

**Risk:** SARS assesses the estate for unpaid taxes, undervaluation, or penalties. The estate cannot be wound up until SARS issues a tax clearance certificate.

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Ensure all tax returns (income tax, provisional tax, VAT) are current for all entities and individuals
- [ ] Keep proper records of all inter-company transactions (especially the director loans — interest must be charged and declared)
- [ ] Conduct a "tax health check" with a tax practitioner — review the last 5 years of returns for errors
- [ ] Ensure the 8% interest on director loans is actually being charged, declared as income in Boerdery, and declared as a deduction in the directors' personal returns

### 6.9 Scenario 9: Meulsteenvlei R12M Obligation

**Risk:** Japie signs a purchase agreement for Meulsteenvlei (processing facility) with R12M due in 2028. If he dies before completion, the estate (or Boerdery) must still perform.

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] Clarify the legal entity that is the purchaser — is it Japie personally, Boerdery, or another entity?
- [ ] If Japie is the purchaser personally, his estate is bound by the contract — the executor must complete or negotiate cancellation
- [ ] Ensure the purchase agreement is in the name of the business entity (Boerdery or Alexenya), not Japie personally
- [ ] Include a clause in the purchase agreement allowing assignment to a related entity
- [ ] R12M life insurance could be taken specifically to cover this obligation
- [ ] Alternative: structured as a sale agreement with a suspensive condition tied to entity continuity

## 7. Where Should Agricultural Properties Sit?

This is the central strategic question. Four options are analysed below.

### 7.1 Option 1: Keep in Nel Familie Trust (Status Quo)

**How it works:** Land remains in IT195/2000. Trust leases to Boerdery. Trust deed governs succession.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Estate duty | **Excellent** — land is not in any individual's estate |
| CGT on death | **Excellent** — no deemed disposal when a trustee dies |
| Transfer costs | **Excellent** — no transfer needed (status quo) |
| Income tax | **Poor if retained** (45%); **Good if distributed** (beneficiary rates) |
| CGT on eventual sale | **Poor** — 36% effective rate |
| Creditor protection | **Good** — provided alter ego risk is managed |
| Governance | **Moderate** — trust deed can be inflexible; trustee concentration risk |
| Succession flexibility | **Moderate** — trust deed amendment possible but needs all trustees to agree |
| SARS reform risk | **High** — trusts are in the crosshairs for tax reform |
| Bringing in investors | **Poor** — trusts are not good vehicles for external investment |
| Compliance burden | **Low-Moderate** — annual tax return, trustee meetings |

**Best for:** Long-term intergenerational land holding where income is distributed to beneficiaries and the trust is properly governed.

### 7.2 Option 2: Move to New Agricultural Holdings (Pty) Ltd

**How it works:** Create a new company. Trust sells/donates land to company. Company leases to Boerdery. Children hold shares (directly or via the trust).

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Estate duty | **Depends** — if shares are held by individuals, they're in the estate. If held by trust, the trust remains the shield. |
| CGT on death | **Depends** — shares held personally = deemed disposal on death. Shares held by trust = no deemed disposal. |
| Transfer costs | **Very expensive upfront** — transfer duty (8% above R2.3M) + CGT on trust's deemed disposal + conveyancing. Estimated cost: **R4M–R7M** |
| Income tax | **Good** — 27% on retained income |
| CGT on eventual sale | **Better** — 21.6% effective (but 41.6% total if distributed as dividend) |
| Creditor protection | **Good** — separate legal personality |
| Governance | **Excellent** — Companies Act provides clear framework; MOI, board, shareholders agreement |
| Succession flexibility | **Excellent** — different share classes (voting, non-voting, preference); can address fair vs equal |
| SARS reform risk | **Low** — companies are the standard structure |
| Bringing in investors | **Excellent** — issue new shares |
| Compliance burden | **Moderate-High** — annual returns, financial statements, CIPC, tax |

**Best for:** Families that want modern governance, the ability to bring in investors, or need to address the fair-vs-equal problem between children through share classes.

**Critical problem:** The **upfront cost of transferring land** from trust to company is prohibitive (R4M–R7M in tax and transfer duty). This makes sense only if the long-term benefits clearly outweigh the costs.

### 7.3 Option 3: Keep in Boerdery BK

**How it works:** Transfer land from trust to Boerdery.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Estate duty | **Terrible** — BK membership interests are personal assets of Japie/Frances and form part of their estates |
| CGT on death | **Terrible** — deemed disposal of membership interest on death |
| Transfer costs | **Expensive** — same as Option 2 (transfer duty + CGT) |
| Creditor protection | **Terrible** — operating entity creditors can now attach the land |
| Succession | **Poor** — BK membership interests are simple; no share classes |
| Overall | **Do NOT pursue this option** |

### 7.4 Option 4: Create New Family Trust with Updated Deed

**How it works:** Wind down IT195/2000 (or keep it dormant). Create a new trust with a modern trust deed addressing all succession, governance, and tax issues. Transfer land from old trust to new trust.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Estate duty | **Excellent** — same as Option 1 |
| CGT on death | **Excellent** — same as Option 1 |
| Transfer costs | **Moderate** — trust-to-trust transfer may qualify for Section 42 relief if structured correctly, but this is complex. Transfer duty may apply. |
| Income tax | **Same as Option 1** — 45% retained, conduit if distributed |
| Governance | **Excellent** — modern trust deed with all the provisions in Section 5 |
| Succession flexibility | **Good** — better than old trust, but still limited vs company |
| Risk | **High execution risk** — SARS may challenge the transfer; the old trust must be properly wound up |

**Best for:** If the current trust deed is fundamentally deficient and cannot be amended.

### 7.5 Recommendation

**Primary recommendation: Keep land in the Nel Familie Trust (Option 1) but upgrade the trust deed and governance.**

Rationale:
1. The estate duty benefit is the single most valuable feature — keeping R30M+ of land out of personal estates saves R5M+ in estate duty at each generational transition
2. The upfront cost of moving to a company (R4M–R7M) is not justified unless the trust structure becomes untenable due to legislative reform
3. The trust's disadvantages (45% retained tax, alter ego risk) can be mitigated through proper governance and income distribution
4. The CGT disadvantage on sale is irrelevant if the land is never sold (intergenerational hold)

**However, combine with a company for operational flexibility:**

Create a **management company** (Cloudskraal Management (Pty) Ltd or similar) that:
- Is owned by the children (or by the trust for estate duty purposes)
- Has a shareholders agreement with all the provisions in Section 5.3
- Manages the farming operations on the trust's land
- Allows share classes (voting vs non-voting) to address fair-vs-equal
- Pays market-rate rent to the trust
- Replaces Boerdery BK over time (or Boerdery converts to Pty Ltd)

This gives the **estate planning benefit of the trust** with the **governance and flexibility of a company**.

| CGT efficiency | - (if sold) | + | + | - |
| Creditor protection | ++ | ++ | --- | ++ |

| Succession flexibility | + | +++ | - | ++ |
