Each goal should be **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) where possible, and the project management system should allow you to break these goals down into tasks and track their progress. A best practice is to document these departmental goals in the system (e.g. on a Notion page or a planning spreadsheet) and link tasks to the goals they contribute to. This creates a clear line of sight from daily to-dos up to Cloudskraal’s strategic vision.

### **Assigning responsibility**:

Make sure every compartment has one person accountable for overseeing progress (even if many people work in that area). For example, Alex might oversee wine/viticulture, Frances coordinate accommodationhandle , Japie manage rooibos, Adri , etc. These owners will report on their domain’s task status and goal progress. This encourages ownership and clarity – everyone knows who is responsible for what domain.

### **Regular review meetings:**

As a management technique, consider holding a brief weekly or bi-weekly meeting with these department owners to review the task register and project progress. In these meetings, you would:

- Go over what tasks were completed (checking them off the list).
- Discuss any tasks that are behind schedule or obstacles encountered.
- Adjust priorities or reassign resources as needed for the coming week.
- Ensure that medium- and long-term projects are making incremental progress (not getting forgotten in day-to-day firefighting).
    
    This habit of **“plan-do-check-act”** (a continuous improvement cycle) keeps the team aligned and problems addressed early. It turns the project management system into a living tool rather than just a static list.
    

### **Metrics and KPIs**:

In addition to tasks and goals, define a few **Key Performance Indicators** for each department that tie into the goals. For instance:

- Vineyard: yield per hectare, wine quality measures, cost per bottle produced.
- Rooibos: kg of tea produced, grade/quality, cost per kg, etc.
- Sheep: lambing percentage, mortality rate, wool clip weight, average daily gain of lambs.
- Accommodation: occupancy rate, guest satisfaction (perhaps via reviews), revenue per month.
- Overall farm finances: monthly cash flow, budget vs actual expenses in each enterprise.
    
    Tracking these metrics (which the new system will help record) lets you quantitatively measure improvements. Farm records are **essential to measure performance over time**, compare with previous years, and identify strong or weak points . The project management system can include a dashboard or at least a manual process to compile these metrics (for example, an Excel or Google Sheet that pulls data from various sources monthly). This way, you ensure the system isn’t just about ticking off tasks, but also about verifying that those tasks are moving the needle on your farm’s success.
    

Overall, structuring the management into clear compartments with responsible owners and well-defined objectives will set the foundation. Now, the next step is to choose the **software tools and platforms** that will enable this structured approach, given Cloudskraal’s unique requirements (offline use, simplicity, integration with farming activities, etc.).
